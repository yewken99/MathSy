# Topic: Consumer Mathematics: Taxation
**Form:** Form 5 | **Chapter:** Chapter 4 | **Curriculum:** SPM KSSM

## Overview
Taxation is the process of collecting revenue from individuals or companies for the development and management of a country. In this topic, students learn about the purpose of taxation, types of taxes in Malaysia, consequences of tax evasion, income tax calculation, road tax, property assessment tax, quit rent, sales and service tax, monthly tax deduction, and joint or separate tax assessment.

## Concept: Taxation
Taxation is a process where the government collects money from individuals or companies to be used for national development and public welfare.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Tax is collected based on laws approved by Parliament.
- Tax is used to provide facilities and services for citizens.
- Tax payment is a compulsory responsibility.
- Tax helps the government manage and develop the country.

### Examples of Tax Usage:
- Education.
- Healthcare.
- Social welfare.
- Infrastructure and public facilities.
- Agriculture and industrial development.
- National security and defence.
- General development.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tax is not only collected to increase government income.
- Tax also helps implement government policies and stabilise the economy.
- Citizens should pay tax honestly because national development may be affected if tax revenue is lost.

## Concept: Purposes of Taxation
There are four main purposes of taxation.

### 1. Source of Government Revenue
Tax is used:
- To govern the country.
- To finance development projects.
- To redistribute income and reduce inequality in society.

### 2. Government Policy Implementation Tool
Tax can support government policy.

Examples:
- Tax relief for parents’ medical expenses encourages a caring society.
- Real Property Gains Tax can reduce excessive property investment activities.
- Digital service tax can create fairer competition between local and foreign service providers.

### 3. Control of Sales of Goods or Services
Tax can discourage people from buying certain goods or services.

Examples:
- Tobacco tax.
- Alcohol tax.
- Gambling tax.

### 4. Financial Tool to Stabilise the Economy
Tax can be used as a financial tool to help stabilise the economy in certain situations.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- If a tax discourages certain behaviour, it is usually related to controlling sales or services.
- If a tax supports a national policy, it is a government policy implementation tool.
- If a tax funds public facilities, it is a source of government revenue.

## Concept: Types of Taxes
The main types of taxes in this chapter are:
- Income tax.
- Road tax.
- Property assessment tax.
- Quit rent.
- Sales and service tax.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Different taxes are collected by different authorities.
- Always identify the type of tax before choosing the formula.
- Some taxes are paid by individuals, while others may be collected by businesses on behalf of the government.

## Concept: Income Tax
Income tax is tax imposed on income earned by an individual or company in Malaysia.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Salaried individuals and profitable companies must register as taxpayers.
- Taxpayers must assess annual income and pay income tax annually.
- Income, exemption, relief, and rebate are reported using the Income Tax Return Form.
- In Malaysia, this form is known as Borang Nyata Cukai Pendapatan, or BNCP.
- Tax can be filed manually or electronically through ezHasil.

### Collector:
Income tax is collected by:

$$
\text{Lembaga Hasil Dalam Negeri (LHDN)}
$$

or Inland Revenue Board, IRB.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Income tax is based on chargeable income, not total income only.
- Some income may be tax-exempt.
- Tax relief and tax rebate are not the same.

## Concept: Self-Assessment System
The self-assessment system means taxpayers are responsible for calculating and reporting their own income tax.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Tax assessment is made for the previous year.
- For example, assessment for year 2019 is made in year 2020.
- Taxpayers must keep documents and original receipts for 7 years.
- Tax payment can be made in one lump sum or through Monthly Tax Deduction.

### Submission Deadlines:
| Taxpayer Type | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Individual without business income | 30 April |
| Individual with business income | 30 June |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Keep receipts because tax relief claims may need proof.
- Tax is assessed for the previous year.
- Self-assessment means the taxpayer must calculate and report honestly.

## Concept: Consequences of Income Tax Evasion
Tax evasion can lead to legal and financial penalties.

| Tax Evasion | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Failure to submit annual report when eligible | Fine of RM200 to RM20 000, imprisonment not exceeding 6 months, or both |
| Omitting income in tax form | Fine of RM1 000 to RM10 000 and penalty of 200% of undercharged tax |
| Giving incorrect information affecting tax payable | Fine of RM1 000 to RM10 000 and penalty of 200% of undercharged tax |
| Intentionally evading tax or helping others evade tax | Fine of RM1 000 to RM20 000, imprisonment not exceeding 3 years, or both, and penalty of 300% of undercharged tax |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tax evasion is not just late payment.
- Giving false information is also tax evasion.
- Penalties may include fine, imprisonment, and additional tax penalty.

## Concept: Road Tax
Road tax is tax imposed on vehicle owners who use vehicles such as motorcycles and cars on the road.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Road tax must be paid before the current road tax expires.
- The road tax expiry date is printed on the road tax document.
- Vehicles without valid road tax are not allowed on the road.
- If road tax has expired for more than one year, the vehicle must be inspected by Puspakom before road tax can be renewed.

### Collector:
Road tax is collected by:

$$
\text{Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan (JPJ)}
$$

### Consequence of Tax Evasion:
If a vehicle is used without valid road tax, the owner may be fined not exceeding RM$2\,000$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Road tax is based on vehicle engine capacity.
- Higher engine capacity usually means higher road tax.
- Use the correct table for motorcycle, private car, Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah, or Sarawak.

## Concept: Property Assessment Tax
Property assessment tax, also called cukai pintu or cukai taksiran, is tax imposed on holdings or properties.

**Examples of Properties:**
- Residential houses.
- Industrial buildings.
- Commercial buildings.
- Vacant land.

### Purpose:
Property assessment tax helps cover municipal expenses such as:
- Rubbish collection.
- Drain cleaning.
- Public park maintenance.
- Local authority services.

### Collector:
Property assessment tax is collected by local authorities such as:
- Municipal council.
- District council.

### Formula:
$$
\text{Property assessment tax}
=
\text{Property assessment tax rate}
\times
\text{Annual value}
$$

where:

$$
\text{Annual value}
=
\text{Estimated monthly rental}
\times
12
$$

### Worked Example:
Madam Kim owns a service apartment. The estimated rent is RM$1\,500$ per month and the property assessment tax rate is $5\%$.

Annual value:

$$
1\,500 \times 12 = 18\,000
$$

Property assessment tax:

$$
5\% \times 18\,000
$$

$$
= 900
$$

Therefore, the tax payable each year is:

$$
\text{RM }900
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Property assessment tax is based on annual value, not property price.
- Convert monthly rent to yearly rent first.
- If the question asks for half-year payment, divide the yearly tax by $2$.

## Concept: Consequences of Property Assessment Tax Evasion
If property assessment tax is not paid, action can be taken by the local authority.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Does not pay within the designated period | Notice in Form E will be issued |
| Does not pay within 15 days after notice | Detention warrant in Form F may be issued and movable property may be seized |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Property assessment tax is collected by local authority, not LHDN.
- If unpaid, movable property may be seized.

## Concept: Quit Rent
Quit rent, also called cukai tanah, is tax imposed on landowners.

**Applies To:**
- Agricultural land.
- Corporate land.
- Land with building.

### Collector:
Quit rent is collected by the state land authority, such as the State Land Office.

### Formula:
$$
\text{Quit rent}
=
\text{Quit rent rate per unit area}
\times
\text{Total land area}
$$

### Worked Example:
Encik Abdullah owns residential land with area $300\text{ m}^2$. The quit rent rate is RM$0.15$ per square metre.

Quit rent:

$$
0.15 \times 300
$$

$$
= 45
$$

Therefore, quit rent payable each year is:

$$
\text{RM }45
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Quit rent is based on land area.
- Property assessment tax is based on annual rental value.
- Quit rent is collected by the state land authority, while property assessment tax is collected by local authority.

## Concept: Consequences of Quit Rent Evasion
If quit rent is not paid, the state land authority may take action.

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Does not pay quit rent within the designated period | Notice of claim in Form 6A will be issued |
| Still does not pay within the stated period | Land can be seized |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Quit rent is related to land ownership.
- If unpaid for the stated period, the land may be seized.

## Concept: Sales and Service Tax
Sales and Service Tax, SST, has two parts:
- Sales tax.
- Service tax.

### Sales Tax
Sales tax is imposed once on taxable goods at:
- Manufacturing stage.
- Importation stage.

### Service Tax
Service tax is imposed on consumers who receive taxable services such as:
- Hotel services.
- Insurance and takaful.
- Food and beverage services.
- Telecommunications.
- Credit cards.

### Collector:
Sales and service tax is collected by:

$$
\text{Jabatan Kastam Diraja Malaysia (JKDM)}
$$

or Royal Malaysian Customs Department.

### Registration Thresholds:
- Manufacturers or importers with taxable goods sales exceeding RM$500\,000$ per year must register under the Sales Tax Act 2018.
- Service providers with taxable services exceeding RM$500\,000$ per year must register under the Service Tax Act 2018.
- Food and beverage service providers must register if taxable services exceed RM$1\,500\,000$ per year.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Service providers collect service tax from customers on behalf of the government.
- Service tax rate in this chapter is $6\%$.
- Sales tax rate can be $5\%$, $10\%$, or other rates depending on the goods.

## Concept: Service Tax Calculation
Service tax is calculated based on the service amount.

### Formula:
$$
\text{Service tax}
=
\text{Service amount}
\times
\text{Service tax rate}
$$

### Worked Example:
Sofia rents a hotel room for RM$240$ per night for $2$ nights. The service tax rate is $6\%$.

Service tax:

$$
240 \times 2 \times 6\%
$$

$$
= 480 \times 0.06
$$

$$
= 28.80
$$

Therefore, the service tax is:

$$
\text{RM }28.80
$$

### Worked Example:
Yi Soon eats:
- Fried rice = RM$6$
- Two pieces of fried chicken = $2 \times \text{RM }5$
- Coffee = RM$4$

Total before tax:

$$
6 + 2(5) + 4 = 20
$$

Service tax:

$$
6\% \times 20 = 1.20
$$

Total bill:

$$
20 + 1.20 = 21.20
$$

Therefore, Yi Soon’s bill is:

$$
\text{RM }21.20
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Add the original price first before calculating service tax.
- To find price including tax, add tax to the original price.
- If the final price already includes tax, use:

$$
\text{Original price} + 6\% \text{ of original price} = \text{Final price}
$$

or:

$$
1.06x = \text{Final price}
$$

## Concept: Consequences of Sales and Service Tax Evasion
Tax evasion under Sales Tax Act and Service Tax Act can involve heavy penalties.

| Tax Evasion | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Intentionally evading tax or helping another person evade tax | First offence: fine of 10 to 20 times the tax amount, imprisonment not exceeding 5 years, or both |
| Second or subsequent offence | Fine of 20 to 40 times the tax amount, imprisonment not exceeding 7 years, or both |
| Tax evasion amount cannot be determined | Fine of RM50 000 to RM500 000, imprisonment not exceeding 7 years, or both |
| Assisting in false tax statements | Fine of RM2 000 to RM20 000, imprisonment not exceeding 3 years, or both |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Sales and service tax evasion can have higher penalties than ordinary late payment.
- The same style of penalty also applies under the Service Tax Act 2018.

## Concept: Chargeable Income
Chargeable income is the income used to calculate income tax.

### Formula:
$$
\text{Chargeable income}
=
\text{Total annual income}
-
\text{Tax exemption}
-
\text{Tax relief}
$$

### Total Annual Income
Total annual income may include:
- Salary.
- Wages.
- Bonus.
- Dividends.
- Interest.
- Rental.
- Royalties.
- Premiums.

Some income may not be taxable, such as:
- Petrol or transport allowance.
- Employer subsidies.
- Medical benefits.
- Certain allowances.

### Tax Exemption
Tax exemption refers to approved expenses or donations that are exempted from tax, subject to limits.

Examples:
- Donations to approved organisations.
- Contributions to state libraries.
- Contributions to healthcare organisations.

### Tax Relief
Tax relief refers to personal or family-related expenses that are not taxed.

Examples:
- Individual relief.
- EPF.
- Life insurance.
- Medical insurance.
- Education fees.
- Medical treatment for parents.

### Worked Example:
Encik Khairul has annual income of RM$125\,300$. Tax-exempt allowances are RM$12\,340$, donation to library is RM$2\,000$, and total tax relief is RM$22\,500$.

Total annual income after exempted allowances:

$$
125\,300 - 12\,340 = 112\,960
$$

Chargeable income:

$$
112\,960 - 2\,000 - 22\,500
$$

$$
= 88\,460
$$

Therefore, chargeable income is:

$$
\text{RM }88\,460
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Subtract tax-exempt income before finding chargeable income.
- Tax exemption and tax relief are both deducted before calculating tax.
- Tax rebate is deducted after income tax is calculated.

## Concept: Income Tax Rates
Income tax is progressive, meaning the tax rate increases as chargeable income increases.

### Individual Income Tax Rates Used in This Chapter

| Chargeable Income | Base Tax | Rate on Next Balance |
|---|---:|---:|
| RM$0$ – RM$5\,000$ | RM$0$ | $0\%$ |
| RM$5\,001$ – RM$20\,000$ | RM$0$ on first RM$5\,000$ | $1\%$ |
| RM$20\,001$ – RM$35\,000$ | RM$150$ on first RM$20\,000$ | $3\%$ |
| RM$35\,001$ – RM$50\,000$ | RM$600$ on first RM$35\,000$ | $8\%$ |
| RM$50\,001$ – RM$70\,000$ | RM$1\,800$ on first RM$50\,000$ | $14\%$ |
| RM$70\,001$ – RM$100\,000$ | RM$4\,600$ on first RM$70\,000$ | $21\%$ |
| RM$100\,001$ – RM$250\,000$ | RM$10\,900$ on first RM$100\,000$ | $24\%$ |
| RM$250\,001$ – RM$400\,000$ | RM$46\,900$ on first RM$250\,000$ | $24.5\%$ |
| RM$400\,001$ – RM$600\,000$ | RM$83\,650$ on first RM$400\,000$ | $25\%$ |
| RM$600\,001$ – RM$1\,000\,000$ | RM$133\,650$ on first RM$600\,000$ | $26\%$ |
| RM$1\,000\,001$ – RM$2\,000\,000$ | RM$237\,650$ on first RM$1\,000\,000$ | $28\%$ |
| Exceeding RM$2\,000\,000$ | RM$517\,650$ on first RM$2\,000\,000$ | $30\%$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not multiply the full chargeable income by the highest rate.
- Use the base tax first, then calculate tax on the balance.
- Choose the correct income range.

## Concept: Tax Rebate
Tax rebate reduces the income tax payable.

**Types of Tax Rebate in This Chapter:**
1. RM$400$ rebate if chargeable income does not exceed RM$35\,000$.
2. Zakat or fitrah paid by Muslim taxpayers.

### Key Difference:
| Item | Deducted From |
|---|---|
| Tax relief | Total annual income |
| Tax rebate | Income tax imposed |

### Worked Example:
Asraf has chargeable income of RM$47\,531$ and paid zakat of RM$280$.

His chargeable income is in the RM$35\,001$ to RM$50\,000$ range.

Tax on first RM$35\,000$:

$$
600
$$

Tax on next balance:

$$
(47\,531 - 35\,000) \times 8\%
$$

$$
= 12\,531 \times 0.08
$$

$$
= 1\,002.48
$$

Income tax payable:

$$
600 + 1\,002.48 - 280
$$

$$
= 1\,322.48
$$

Therefore, income tax payable is:

$$
\text{RM }1\,322.48
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- RM$400$ rebate applies only when chargeable income is not more than RM$35\,000$.
- Zakat rebate is subtracted from the tax amount.
- If rebate is more than tax, tax payable becomes RM$0$; excess rebate is not refunded.

## Concept: Calculating Income Tax Payable
To calculate income tax payable:

1. Calculate chargeable income.
2. Identify the correct tax bracket.
3. Use base tax for the first part of income.
4. Calculate tax on the remaining balance.
5. Subtract eligible tax rebate.
6. State the final income tax payable.

### General Form:
$$
\text{Income tax payable}
=
\text{Base tax}
+
\text{Tax on next balance}
-
\text{Tax rebate}
$$

### Worked Example:
Mr Chan has total annual income RM$52\,770$, donation RM$300$, and tax reliefs:
- Individual = RM$9\,000$
- Life insurance and EPF = RM$5\,800$
- Medical insurance = RM$1\,700$
- Self-education fees = RM$4\,000$

Chargeable income:

$$
52\,770 - 300 - (9\,000 + 5\,800 + 1\,700 + 4\,000)
$$

$$
= 31\,970
$$

Since RM$31\,970$ is not more than RM$35\,000$, he is eligible for RM$400$ rebate.

Tax on first RM$20\,000$:

$$
150
$$

Tax on next balance:

$$
(31\,970 - 20\,000) \times 3\%
$$

$$
= 11\,970 \times 0.03
$$

$$
= 359.10
$$

Income tax payable:

$$
150 + 359.10 - 400
$$

$$
= 109.10
$$

Therefore, income tax payable is:

$$
\text{RM }109.10
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Remember to subtract donations if they are approved tax exemptions.
- Apply tax rebate only after calculating tax.
- Do not apply the RM$400$ rebate if chargeable income exceeds RM$35\,000$.

## Concept: Monthly Tax Deduction
Monthly Tax Deduction, or PCB, is a monthly salary deduction used to pay income tax for the current year.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- PCB is deducted from salary every month.
- At the end of the year, compare total PCB with actual tax payable.
- If tax payable is greater than PCB, the taxpayer must pay the balance.
- If PCB is greater than tax payable, the excess may be refunded.

### Formula:
If:

$$
\text{Tax payable} > \text{PCB}
$$

then:

$$
\text{Insufficient tax payment}
=
\text{Tax payable}
-
\text{PCB}
$$

If:

$$
\text{Tax payable} < \text{PCB}
$$

then:

$$
\text{Excess deduction}
=
\text{PCB}
-
\text{Tax payable}
$$

### Worked Example:
Mr Lim’s income tax payable is RM$2\,419.50$. His PCB is RM$180$ per month.

Total PCB:

$$
180 \times 12 = 2\,160
$$

Since:

$$
2\,419.50 > 2\,160
$$

insufficient tax payment:

$$
2\,419.50 - 2\,160 = 259.50
$$

Therefore, Mr Lim needs to pay:

$$
\text{RM }259.50
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- PCB is not the final tax amount.
- Always multiply monthly PCB by $12$ to get yearly PCB.
- Compare PCB with actual tax payable.

## Concept: Joint Tax Assessment and Separate Tax Assessment
For married individuals, income tax can be assessed using:
- Joint tax assessment.
- Separate tax assessment.

### Joint Tax Assessment
Joint tax assessment means the income of husband and wife is combined and filed under one assessment.

### Separate Tax Assessment
Separate tax assessment means husband and wife each file their own tax assessment.

### Key Idea:
If both husband and wife have income, separate tax assessment is often more suitable because tax relief limits can be claimed separately.

### Worked Example Summary:
Mr Rajan and his wife have annual incomes of RM$53\,000$ and RM$57\,000$.

Using joint assessment:
- Chargeable income = RM$87\,500$
- Income tax payable = RM$8\,275$

Using separate assessment:
- Husband’s tax = RM$125.60$
- Wife’s tax = RM$714.40$
- Total tax = RM$840$

Therefore, separate tax assessment is better because:

$$
840 < 8\,275
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- In joint assessment, claimable tax relief is considered once only.
- In separate assessment, each spouse calculates tax separately.
- Always compare total tax payable if the question asks which method is better.

## Concept: Road Tax Calculation
Road tax depends on the engine capacity of the vehicle.

### Motorcycle Example:
If a motorcycle has engine capacity $220$ cc and the table states:
- $201$ cc to $250$ cc = RM$50$

Then road tax is:

$$
\text{RM }50
$$

### Private Car Example:
A private car has engine capacity $1\,860$ cc.

For $1\,801$ cc to $2\,000$ cc:
- Base rate = RM$280$
- Progressive rate = RM$0.50$ for each cc exceeding $1\,800$ cc

Excess cc:

$$
1\,860 - 1\,800 = 60
$$

Road tax:

$$
280 + 0.50(60)
$$

$$
= 280 + 30
$$

$$
= 310
$$

Therefore, road tax is:

$$
\text{RM }310
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use only the excess engine capacity for progressive rate.
- Do not multiply the full engine capacity by the progressive rate.
- Check whether the vehicle is a motorcycle or private car.

## Concept: Solving Problems Involving Taxation
Taxation problems usually require identifying the type of tax and applying the correct formula.

### Problem-Solving Steps:
1. Identify the type of tax.
2. Extract the important values.
3. Choose the correct formula.
4. Substitute values carefully.
5. Apply exemptions, reliefs, or rebates if needed.
6. State the final answer with RM and context.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- For income tax, always calculate chargeable income first.
- For road tax, identify the vehicle type and engine capacity range.
- For property assessment tax, use annual value.
- For quit rent, use land area.
- For service tax, use the service amount and tax rate.

## Concept: Electricity Bill and Service Tax
Some electricity bills may include service tax only on certain usage.

### Worked Example:
Mr Khor uses $650$ kWh of electricity. Usage exceeding $600$ kWh is subjected to $6\%$ service tax.

The table gives:
- First $200$ kWh at RM$0.218$
- Next $100$ kWh at RM$0.334$
- Next $300$ kWh at RM$0.516$
- Next $50$ kWh at RM$0.546$

Calculate:

$$
x = 100(0.334) = 33.40
$$

$$
y = 300(0.516) = 154.80
$$

$$
z = 50(0.546) = 27.30
$$

Amount not subjected to service tax:

$$
43.60 + 33.40 + 154.80 = 231.80
$$

Service tax charged:

$$
27.30 \times 6\% = 1.64
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Only the taxable portion is charged service tax.
- Read tariff blocks carefully.
- Calculate each block separately.

## Concept: Summary of Key Formulas

### Chargeable Income
$$
\text{Chargeable income}
=
\text{Total annual income}
-
\text{Tax exemption}
-
\text{Tax relief}
$$

### Income Tax Payable
$$
\text{Income tax payable}
=
\text{Base tax}
+
\text{Tax on next balance}
-
\text{Tax rebate}
$$

### Tax on Next Balance
$$
\text{Tax on next balance}
=
(\text{Chargeable income} - \text{First income limit})
\times
\text{Tax rate}
$$

### Total PCB
$$
\text{Total PCB}
=
\text{Monthly PCB}
\times
12
$$

### Insufficient Tax Payment
$$
\text{Insufficient tax payment}
=
\text{Tax payable}
-
\text{Total PCB}
$$

### Excess PCB Deduction
$$
\text{Excess deduction}
=
\text{Total PCB}
-
\text{Tax payable}
$$

### Property Assessment Tax
$$
\text{Property assessment tax}
=
\text{Property assessment tax rate}
\times
\text{Annual value}
$$

### Annual Value
$$
\text{Annual value}
=
\text{Estimated monthly rental}
\times
12
$$

### Quit Rent
$$
\text{Quit rent}
=
\text{Quit rent rate per unit area}
\times
\text{Total land area}
$$

### Service Tax
$$
\text{Service tax}
=
\text{Service amount}
\times
\text{Service tax rate}
$$

### Price Including Service Tax
$$
\text{Final price}
=
\text{Original price}
+
\text{Service tax}
$$

or, for $6\%$ service tax:

$$
\text{Final price}
=
1.06 \times \text{Original price}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tax relief reduces chargeable income.
- Tax rebate reduces tax payable.
- Zakat is treated as a tax rebate.
- RM$400$ rebate applies only if chargeable income does not exceed RM$35\,000$.
- For road tax, calculate progressive charge only on the excess cc.
- For property assessment tax, use annual rental value.
- For quit rent, use land area.
- For service tax, check whether the price is before tax or already includes tax.
