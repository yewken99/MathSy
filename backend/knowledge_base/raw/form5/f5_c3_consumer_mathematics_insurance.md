# Topic: Consumer Mathematics: Insurance
**Form:** Form 5 | **Chapter:** Chapter 3 | **Curriculum:** SPM KSSM

## Overview
Insurance is a financial protection system used to reduce the financial burden caused by uncertain losses. In this topic, students learn about risk, insurance coverage, life insurance, general insurance, premiums, motor insurance, deductible, co-insurance, and how to solve insurance-related problems.

## Concept: Risk
Risk is the possibility of a disaster, accident, loss, or unwanted event that cannot be fully avoided.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Risk is uncertain.
- Risk may involve financial loss.
- Everyone is exposed to risk in daily life.
- Risk can happen to a person, family, property, vehicle, business, or health.

### Examples of Risk:
- Road accident.
- Fire damaging a house.
- Theft of a motorcycle.
- High medical bills due to illness.
- Injury while playing sports.
- Damage to property due to flood or lightning.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Risk does not mean the loss has already happened.
- Risk means there is a possibility of loss.
- In insurance, the risk should usually be measurable in money.

## Concept: Insurance
Insurance is a contract between a policyholder and an insurance company where the policyholder pays a premium, and the insurance company pays compensation for losses covered by the policy.

**Key Terms:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insurance company | The company that provides protection and pays compensation |
| Policyholder | The person who owns the insurance policy |
| Insurance policy | The contract between the policyholder and insurance company |
| Premium | The payment made by the policyholder to the insurance company |
| Coverage | The protection provided by the insurance policy |
| Compensation | The amount paid by the insurance company when a covered loss occurs |

### Key Rules / Methods:
- Insurance transfers risk from an individual to an insurance company.
- Insurance does not prevent loss from happening.
- Insurance reduces the financial burden after a loss occurs.
- The policyholder must pay a premium to receive protection.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Insurance is not a way to earn profit.
- Insurance only covers risks stated in the policy.
- Always read the policy terms before deciding what can be claimed.

## Concept: Principle of Indemnity
The principle of indemnity means the insurance company pays compensation to restore the policyholder’s financial position to the condition before the loss happened.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Compensation is based on the actual loss suffered.
- The policyholder cannot receive more than the loss suffered.
- The policyholder cannot gain profit from insurance.
- The maximum compensation is subject to the sum insured and policy terms.

### Example:
If a policyholder suffers a covered loss of RM$5\,500$ and the deductible is RM$200$, the insurance company may pay:

$$
5\,500 - 200 = 5\,300
$$

The policyholder bears RM$200$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Compensation is not always equal to the total loss.
- Deductible and co-insurance may reduce the compensation.
- The insurance company will not pay more than the actual loss.

## Concept: Importance of Insurance
Insurance is important because it gives financial protection against unexpected losses.

**Key Reasons to Buy Insurance:**
- Provides financial aid to family in case of death, disability, or critical illness.
- Helps manage living expenses, debts, and commitments if unable to work.
- Helps pay high medical expenses.
- Provides compensation for covered losses.
- Reduces uncertainty caused by financial loss.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Insurance does not remove risk completely.
- Insurance reduces the financial effect of risk.
- A person should choose insurance based on needs and financial ability.

## Concept: Life Insurance
Life insurance guarantees payment of a stated amount of money when the insured person dies or experiences certain conditions stated in the contract.

**Risks Covered by Life Insurance:**
- Death.
- Critical illness.
- Loss of ability.
- Total and permanent disability, depending on policy terms.

### Purpose:
Life insurance provides financial protection to family members who depend on the policyholder.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Life insurance mainly protects people, not property.
- Life insurance compensation may be paid to beneficiaries.
- Life insurance is different from medical insurance and personal accident insurance.

## Concept: General Insurance
General insurance provides coverage against losses or damage other than those covered by life insurance.

**Types of General Insurance:**
- Motor insurance.
- Fire insurance.
- Medical and health insurance.
- Personal accident insurance.
- Travel insurance.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- General insurance usually protects property, health, travel, accident, or liability risks.
- Most general insurance policies have a coverage period of one year or less.
- Premiums for general insurance are usually paid once for the coverage period.

## Concept: Motor Insurance
Motor insurance provides coverage against loss or damage related to the use of a motor vehicle.

**Types of Motor Insurance Policies:**

| Coverage | Act | Third party | Third party, fire & theft | Comprehensive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Liability to third party due to injury and death | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Loss of property suffered by third party | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Loss to own vehicle due to accidental fire or theft | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Loss and damage to own vehicle due to accident | No | No | No | Yes |

### Key Rules / Methods:
- Comprehensive policy gives the widest protection.
- Third party policy protects against claims by third parties.
- Third party, fire and theft policy adds protection for own vehicle due to fire or theft.
- Motor insurance does not automatically cover injury or death claims by the driver and passengers.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- “Third party” does not mean the policyholder.
- In motor insurance:
  - First party = driver or policyholder.
  - Second party = insurance company.
  - Third party = other person involved in an accident caused by the policyholder.
- Comprehensive insurance is broader than third party insurance.

## Concept: Fire Insurance
Fire insurance provides coverage against damage caused by fire, lightning, and explosion at a house or business premise.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Compensation is paid subject to the sum insured.
- Additional coverage may be added with extra premium.
- Additional coverage may include hurricane, flood, riot, and other risks.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Basic fire insurance does not automatically cover every disaster.
- Read whether additional coverage is included.
- Fire insurance is commonly linked with co-insurance questions.

## Concept: Medical and Health Insurance
Medical and health insurance provides coverage for medical expenses.

**Examples of Policies:**
- Hospitalisation and surgical insurance.
- Critical illness insurance.
- Disability income insurance.
- Hospital income insurance.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Medical and health insurance helps cover treatment costs.
- Critical illness insurance may be an add-on to a basic policy.
- Deductible and co-insurance are common in health insurance problems.

## Concept: Personal Accident Insurance
Personal accident insurance provides coverage if the policyholder suffers bodily injury, disability, or death directly caused by an accident.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Personal accident insurance is not the same as life insurance.
- It usually requires the injury, disability, or death to result directly from an accident.
- Occupation risk can affect the premium.

## Concept: Travel Insurance
Travel insurance protects the policyholder against losses during travel by land, air, or sea.

**Possible Coverage:**
- Death.
- Permanent disability.
- Loss of luggage.
- Loss of passport.
- Loss of money.
- Medical expenses during travel.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Travel insurance premium may depend on destination, duration, and number of people insured.
- Longer travel periods usually have higher premiums because the probability of loss increases.
- Annual travel insurance may be cheaper for frequent travellers.

## Concept: Group Insurance
Group insurance provides coverage to a group of individuals.

**Examples:**
- Employees in a company.
- Pupils in school.
- Students in educational institutions.

### Group Insurance for Organisations:
Provides financial protection to employees in events such as:
- Death.
- Disability.
- Hospitalisation.
- Surgery.

### Group Insurance for Pupils:
Provides financial protection to pupils in events such as:
- Death.
- Paralysis.
- Disability.
- Wheelchair allowance.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Group insurance covers a group, not just one individual.
- Coverage limits depend on the policy.
- School pupils may be protected under student takaful schemes.

## Concept: Factors Affecting Insurance Premiums
Insurance premiums may differ depending on the type of insurance and level of risk.

### Common Factors:
- Age.
- Gender.
- Smoking status.
- Occupation risk.
- Health condition.
- Type of coverage.
- Amount of coverage.
- Travel destination.
- Travel duration.
- Number of people insured.
- Vehicle engine capacity.
- Vehicle market value.
- Location of vehicle use.
- No Claim Discount.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Higher risk usually means higher premium.
- A smoker usually pays a higher life insurance premium than a non-smoker.
- Older age usually increases life insurance premium because life expectancy decreases.
- Manual work or machinery-related jobs usually have higher accident insurance premiums.

## Concept: Premium for Life Insurance
For life insurance, premium is calculated based on the face value of the policy and the premium rate.

**Formula:**

$$
\text{Premium}
=
\frac{\text{Face value of policy}}{\text{RM}x}
\times
\text{Premium rate per RM}x
$$

Usually, the premium rate is given per RM$1\,000$ face value.

### Worked Example:
Mr Guan wants to buy an insurance policy worth RM$100\,000$. He is $39$ years old, healthy, and a non-smoker. The premium rate is RM$2.49$ per RM$1\,000$.

Annual premium:

$$
\frac{100\,000}{1\,000} \times 2.49
$$

$$
= 100 \times 2.49
$$

$$
= 249
$$

Therefore, Mr Guan’s annual premium is:

$$
\text{RM }249.00
$$

### Worked Example:
Madam Shapuva wants to buy a policy worth RM$250\,000$. She is $36$ years old, healthy, and a non-smoker. The basic premium rate is RM$1.50$ per RM$1\,000$.

She also adds critical illness coverage of $30\%$ of the basic face value. The critical illness premium rate is RM$1.77$ per RM$1\,000$.

Critical illness coverage:

$$
30\% \times 250\,000
$$

$$
= 75\,000
$$

Basic annual premium:

$$
\frac{250\,000}{1\,000} \times 1.50
$$

$$
= 375
$$

Additional critical illness premium:

$$
\frac{75\,000}{1\,000} \times 1.77
$$

$$
= 132.75
$$

Total annual premium:

$$
375 + 132.75 = 507.75
$$

Therefore, Madam Shapuva’s annual premium is:

$$
\text{RM }507.75
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use the correct premium rate based on age, gender, and smoking status.
- Divide the face value by RM$1\,000$ if the rate is per RM$1\,000$.
- For add-on coverage, calculate the add-on coverage value first.

## Concept: Premium for Travel Insurance
Travel insurance premiums are usually given in a table.

**Factors Affecting Travel Insurance Premiums:**
- Destination.
- Duration of travel.
- Number of people insured.

### Example Interpretation:
A person travelling to Europe for a longer period usually pays more than a person travelling to Asia for a shorter period.

A frequent traveller who visits many countries in a year may choose annual travel insurance because it may be more economical.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Read the correct row for number of days.
- Read the correct column for destination and insured group.
- Annual premium is suitable when the person travels frequently.

## Concept: Premium for Motor Insurance
Motor insurance premium depends on the type of policy, engine capacity, sum insured, location, and No Claim Discount.

### Types of Motor Policy Premium:
- Act policy: depends only on engine capacity.
- Third party policy: depends only on engine capacity.
- Third party, fire and theft policy: usually based on $75\%$ of comprehensive basic premium.
- Comprehensive policy: depends on engine capacity and market value or sum insured.

### Comprehensive Policy Formula for Peninsular Malaysia:
$$
\text{Basic premium}
=
\text{Rate for first RM}1\,000
+
26 \times
\text{number of RM}1\,000\text{ or part thereof above first RM}1\,000
$$

### Comprehensive Policy Formula for Sabah and Sarawak:
$$
\text{Basic premium}
=
\text{Rate for first RM}1\,000
+
20.30 \times
\text{number of RM}1\,000\text{ or part thereof above first RM}1\,000
$$

### Gross Premium After NCD:
$$
\text{Gross premium}
=
\text{Basic premium}
-
\text{NCD amount}
$$

where:

$$
\text{NCD amount}
=
\text{NCD rate} \times \text{Basic premium}
$$

### Worked Example:
Encik Ramli owns a Proton Exora in Peninsular Malaysia.

Information:
- Sum insured = RM$60\,000$
- Engine capacity = $1\,600$ cc
- NCD = $25\%$
- Comprehensive policy rate for first RM$1\,000$ = RM$305.50$

Amount exceeding first RM$1\,000$:

$$
60\,000 - 1\,000 = 59\,000
$$

Number of RM$1\,000$ units:

$$
\frac{59\,000}{1\,000} = 59
$$

Extra premium:

$$
26 \times 59 = 1\,534
$$

Basic premium:

$$
305.50 + 1\,534 = 1\,839.50
$$

NCD:

$$
25\% \times 1\,839.50 = 459.88
$$

Gross premium:

$$
1\,839.50 - 459.88 = 1\,379.62
$$

Therefore, the gross premium is:

$$
\text{RM }1\,379.62
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use the correct engine capacity row in the tariff table.
- For comprehensive policy, remember to include the value exceeding the first RM$1\,000$.
- Use the correct NCD percentage.
- Third party, fire and theft policy uses $75\%$ of comprehensive basic premium before deducting NCD.
- Premium payable may still differ because of service tax, stamp duty, agent commission, and other charges.

## Concept: No Claim Discount
No Claim Discount, or NCD, reduces the premium if no claim has been made during the coverage period before renewal.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- NCD reduces the premium payable.
- NCD is lost if a personal or third-party claim is made.
- NCD eligibility depends on vehicle class and claim-free driving history.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- NCD is a discount, so subtract it from the basic premium.
- Do not add NCD to the premium.
- NCD is calculated using the basic premium of the policy being considered.

## Concept: Deductible
Deductible is the amount that must be borne by the policyholder before making a claim from the insurance company.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If the loss is less than or equal to the deductible, no compensation is paid.
- If the loss is more than the deductible, compensation is:

$$
\text{Compensation} = \text{Amount of loss} - \text{Deductible}
$$

### Worked Example:
Madam Suhaila has motor insurance with a deductible of RM$300$.

| Month | Loss | Can claim? | Compensation |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| March | RM$2\,800$ | Yes | RM$2\,500$ |
| July | RM$250$ | No | RM$0$ |
| August | RM$400$ | Yes | RM$100$ |

March:

$$
2\,800 - 300 = 2\,500
$$

July:

$$
250 < 300
$$

No claim can be made.

August:

$$
400 - 300 = 100
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Deductible is paid by the policyholder.
- The insurance company only pays the amount after deductible.
- If loss is below deductible, the policyholder bears the full loss.

## Concept: Deductible in Medical Insurance
For medical insurance, the policyholder must bear the deductible amount first before the insurance company pays.

### Worked Example:
Cher Lin has medical insurance with:
- Deductible = RM$30\,000$ per year.
- Annual limit = RM$300\,000$.

First year treatment cost:

$$
\text{RM }8\,000
$$

Since RM$8\,000 < RM$30\,000$, Cher Lin bears the full RM$8\,000$.

Second year treatment cost:

$$
\text{RM }210\,000
$$

Cher Lin bears:

$$
\text{RM }30\,000
$$

Insurance company pays:

$$
210\,000 - 30\,000 = 180\,000
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Deductible may apply yearly.
- Check the annual limit.
- The insurance company pays only after deductible is considered.

## Concept: Co-insurance in Property Insurance
Co-insurance is cost sharing between the insurance company and the policyholder.

In property insurance, the policyholder must insure the property at a certain percentage of its insurable value.

### Amount of Required Insurance:
$$
\text{Amount of required insurance}
=
\text{Percentage of co-insurance}
\times
\text{Insurable value of property}
$$

### Case 1: Insured Value Equals Required Insurance
If the insured value equals the required insurance:

$$
\text{Compensation}
=
\text{Amount of loss}
-
\text{Deductible}
$$

where the amount of loss is less than the amount of insurance purchased.

### Case 2: Insured Value Is Less Than Required Insurance
If the insured value is less than the amount of required insurance:

$$
\text{Compensation}
=
\frac{\text{Amount of insurance purchased}}{\text{Amount of required insurance}}
\times
\text{Amount of loss}
-
\text{Deductible}
$$

### Case 3: Total Loss
If the property suffers total loss:

$$
\text{Compensation}
=
\text{Amount of insurance purchased}
-
\text{Deductible}
$$

### Worked Example:
Encik Ismail wants to buy fire insurance for his house.

Information:
- Insurable value = RM$350\,000$
- Co-insurance provision = $80\%$
- Deductible = RM$2\,000$

Amount of required insurance:

$$
80\% \times 350\,000
$$

$$
= 280\,000
$$

So Encik Ismail should insure his house for:

$$
\text{RM }280\,000
$$

### Worked Example: Insured at Required Amount
Loss = RM$25\,000$

Compensation:

$$
25\,000 - 2\,000 = 23\,000
$$

Therefore, compensation is:

$$
\text{RM }23\,000
$$

### Worked Example: Underinsured
Encik Ismail only insures his house for RM$150\,000$.

Since:

$$
150\,000 < 280\,000
$$

he is underinsured.

Compensation:

$$
\frac{150\,000}{280\,000} \times 25\,000 - 2\,000
$$

$$
= 13\,392.86 - 2\,000
$$

$$
= 11\,392.86
$$

Therefore, compensation is:

$$
\text{RM }11\,392.86
$$

Co-insurance penalty:

$$
25\,000 - 13\,392.86 = 11\,607.14
$$

### Worked Example: Total Loss
If Encik Ismail insured his house for RM$200\,000$ and the house suffered total loss:

$$
\text{Compensation} = 200\,000 - 2\,000
$$

$$
= 198\,000
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- First calculate the amount of required insurance.
- If the policyholder buys less than the required amount, use the co-insurance formula.
- Deductible is subtracted after applying the co-insurance fraction.
- For total loss, compensation is based on the amount insured minus deductible.
- Co-insurance penalty happens when the policyholder does not meet the required insurance amount.

## Concept: Co-insurance in Health Insurance
In health insurance, co-insurance means the policyholder shares medical costs with the insurance company based on an agreed percentage.

### Example:
A $80/20$ co-insurance clause means:
- Insurance company pays $80\%$.
- Policyholder pays $20\%$.

If there is a deductible, subtract the deductible first before applying the co-insurance percentage.

### Worked Example:
Madam Chen has:
- Medical cost = RM$20\,600$
- Deductible = RM$500$
- Co-insurance percentage = $75/25$

Medical cost after deductible:

$$
20\,600 - 500 = 20\,100
$$

Insurance company pays:

$$
75\% \times 20\,100
$$

$$
= 15\,075
$$

Madam Chen pays:

$$
25\% \times 20\,100 + 500
$$

$$
= 5\,025 + 500
$$

$$
= 5\,525
$$

Therefore:
- Insurance company pays RM$15\,075$.
- Madam Chen pays RM$5\,525$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Deductible is paid first by the policyholder.
- Co-insurance percentage applies after deductible.
- For $75/25$, the first percentage usually refers to the insurance company’s share.

## Concept: Difference Between Deductible and Co-insurance
Deductible and co-insurance both reduce the amount paid by the insurance company, but they work differently.

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deductible | Fixed amount paid first by the policyholder |
| Co-insurance | Percentage sharing of cost between policyholder and insurance company |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Deductible is usually a fixed amount such as RM$300$.
- Co-insurance is usually a percentage such as $80/20$ or $75/25$.
- Some policies may have both deductible and co-insurance.

## Concept: Choosing the Best Insurance Policy
A good insurance choice depends on coverage, premium, benefits, and personal needs.

**Guidelines:**
1. Find out the total coverage needed.
2. Compare premium rates and insurance benefits.
3. Understand the scope of coverage.
4. Understand the terms and conditions of the policy.
5. Avoid unnecessary coverage.

### Worked Example:
Two medical insurance plans have the same overall annual limit of RM$50\,000$.

Plan XX:
- Annual premium = RM$506.02$
- Hospital room and board = RM$160$ for up to $365$ days.
- ICU = as charged, up to $90$ days.
- Government hospital cash allowance = RM$100$ for up to $365$ days.

Plan YY:
- Annual premium = RM$637.02$
- Hospital room and board = RM$200$ for up to $200$ days.
- ICU = RM$400$ for up to $90$ days.
- Government hospital cash allowance = RM$50$ for up to $200$ days.

Plan XX is better because:
- Both plans have the same overall annual limit.
- Plan XX has a lower annual premium.
- Plan XX has longer coverage periods for hospital room and board and government hospital cash allowance.
- Plan XX pays ICU charges as charged, while YY has an ICU limit.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not choose a plan based only on lower premium.
- Compare benefits, coverage period, limits, and exclusions.
- The best plan depends on the policyholder’s needs.

## Concept: Solving Insurance Word Problems
Insurance word problems usually require identifying the policy type, formula, and correct values.

**Problem-Solving Steps:**
1. Identify the type of insurance.
2. Identify whether the question involves premium, deductible, or co-insurance.
3. Extract the important values.
4. Choose the correct formula.
5. Substitute carefully.
6. State the final answer with RM and context.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Check whether the question asks for premium, compensation, amount borne, or amount insured.
- For life insurance premium, use the face value formula.
- For property co-insurance, calculate required insurance first.
- For health co-insurance, deduct deductible first, then apply percentage sharing.
- For motor insurance, use the correct tariff row and NCD.
