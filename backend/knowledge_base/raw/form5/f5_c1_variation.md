# Topic: Variation
**Form:** Form 5 | **Chapter:** Chapter 1 | **Curriculum:** SPM KSSM

## Overview
Variation describes how one quantity changes in relation to another quantity. In this topic, students learn about direct variation, inverse variation, joint variation, and combined variation. These concepts are used in real-life situations involving distance, speed, time, pressure, volume, interest, force, area, and other related quantities.

## Concept: Variable, Constant, and Variation
A variable is a quantity that can change, while a constant is a fixed value.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- A variable can take different values.
- A constant remains unchanged.
- Variation describes the relationship between variables.
- The symbol $\propto$ means “is proportional to”.
- A constant of proportionality is usually written as $k$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not treat $k$ as a variable. It is a constant for the same relationship.
- The symbol $\propto$ is used for the variation relation.
- The equation form uses $=$ and includes the constant $k$.

## Concept: Direct Variation
Direct variation explains the relationship between two variables where both variables change in the same direction at the same rate.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $y$ increases when $x$ increases, and $y$ decreases when $x$ decreases at the same rate, then $y$ varies directly as $x$.
- This is written as:

$$
y \propto x
$$

- The equation form is:

$$
y = kx
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

- The value of $k$ can be found using:

$$
k = \frac{y}{x}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Direct variation means the ratio $\frac{y}{x}$ is constant.
- If $\frac{y}{x}$ is not constant, then $y$ does not vary directly as $x$.
- The graph of $y$ against $x$ must be a straight line passing through the origin.

## Concept: Change in Direct Variation
In direct variation, both quantities change in the same direction.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $x$ is doubled, then $y$ is doubled.
- If $x$ is halved, then $y$ is halved.
- If $x$ increases by $10\%$, then $y$ increases by $10\%$.
- If $x$ decreases by $40\%$, then $y$ decreases by $40\%$.

### Worked Example:
The total wage of a part-time worker varies directly as the number of hours worked.

(a) If the number of working hours is doubled, the total wage is doubled.

(b) If the number of working hours decreases by $40\%$, the total wage decreases by $40\%$.

(c) If the total wage is halved, the number of working hours is halved.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- For direct variation, both variables change at the same rate.
- Do not reverse the change as in inverse variation.
- “Varies directly” means the two quantities increase or decrease together.

## Concept: Determining Direct Variation from a Table
A table can be used to determine whether two variables have direct variation.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Calculate $\frac{y}{x}$ for each pair of values.
- If all values of $\frac{y}{x}$ are the same, then $y$ varies directly as $x$.
- If not all values are the same, then $y$ does not vary directly as $x$.

### Worked Example:
Determine whether $y$ varies directly as $t$ or $t^2$.

| $t$ | $2$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ | $10$ | $12$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $y$ | $14$ | $28$ | $42$ | $56$ | $70$ | $84$ |

Check $\frac{y}{t}$:

$$
\frac{14}{2} = 7
$$

$$
\frac{28}{4} = 7
$$

$$
\frac{42}{6} = 7
$$

Since $\frac{y}{t}$ is constant:

$$
y \propto t
$$

Check $\frac{y}{t^2}$:

$$
\frac{14}{2^2} = 3.5
$$

$$
\frac{28}{4^2} = 1.75
$$

Since $\frac{y}{t^2}$ is not constant, $y$ does not vary directly as $t^2$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Always check the ratio that matches the proposed relation.
- For $y \propto x^2$, check $\frac{y}{x^2}$, not $\frac{y}{x}$.
- For $y \propto \sqrt{x}$, check $\frac{y}{\sqrt{x}}$.

## Concept: Direct Variation with Powers
A variable can vary directly as a power or root of another variable.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
For direct variation:

$$
y \propto x^n
$$

The equation form is:

$$
y = kx^n
$$

where:

$$
n = 1, 2, 3, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{3}
$$

and $k$ is a constant.

Examples:
- $y$ varies directly as $x$:

$$
y \propto x
$$

$$
y = kx
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the square of $x$:

$$
y \propto x^2
$$

$$
y = kx^2
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the cube of $x$:

$$
y \propto x^3
$$

$$
y = kx^3
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the square root of $x$:

$$
y \propto \sqrt{x}
$$

$$
y = k\sqrt{x}
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the cube root of $x$:

$$
y \propto \sqrt[3]{x}
$$

$$
y = k\sqrt[3]{x}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- $y \propto x$ is only true when $n = 1$.
- If $y \propto x^2$, then $y$ does not vary directly as $x$.
- Always identify whether the question says $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$, or $\sqrt[3]{x}$.

## Concept: Graph of Direct Variation
The graph of a direct variation is a straight line through the origin when the correct horizontal variable is used.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $y \propto x$, the graph of $y$ against $x$ is a straight line through the origin.
- If $y \propto x^2$, the graph of $y$ against $x^2$ is a straight line through the origin.
- If $y \propto x^n$, the graph of $y$ against $x^n$ is a straight line through the origin.
- The gradient of the straight line is the constant of proportionality, $k$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A straight line that does not pass through the origin does not represent direct variation.
- The graph must be drawn using the correct horizontal variable.
- For $y \propto x^2$, plot $y$ against $x^2$, not $y$ against $x$.

## Concept: Expressing a Direct Variation Equation
To express one variable in terms of another, write the variation relation as an equation and find $k$.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
1. Write the variation relation.
2. Change it into equation form using $k$.
3. Substitute the given values to find $k$.
4. Write the final equation.

### Worked Example:
It is given that $m = 12$ when $n = 3$. Express $m$ in terms of $n$ if $m$ varies directly as $n$.

Since:

$$
m \propto n
$$

write:

$$
m = kn
$$

Substitute $m = 12$ and $n = 3$:

$$
12 = k(3)
$$

$$
k = 4
$$

Therefore:

$$
m = 4n
$$

### Worked Example:
It is given that $m = 12$ when $n = 3$. Express $m$ in terms of $n$ if $m$ varies directly as $n^3$.

Since:

$$
m \propto n^3
$$

write:

$$
m = kn^3
$$

Substitute $m = 12$ and $n = 3$:

$$
12 = k(3^3)
$$

$$
12 = 27k
$$

$$
k = \frac{4}{9}
$$

Therefore:

$$
m = \frac{4}{9}n^3
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not forget to substitute into the equation form.
- Use the given values only to find $k$.
- After finding $k$, write the final equation in terms of the variables.

## Concept: Solving Direct Variation Problems
Direct variation problems can be solved after the equation is formed.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Form the equation first.
- Find $k$ using the given condition.
- Substitute the required value to calculate the unknown.
- Use suitable units in the final answer.

### Worked Example:
The extension of a spring, $x$ cm, varies directly as the mass of the load, $w$ g. The extension is $3$ cm when the load is $200$ g. Express $x$ in terms of $w$.

Since:

$$
x \propto w
$$

write:

$$
x = kw
$$

Substitute $x = 3$ and $w = 200$:

$$
3 = k(200)
$$

$$
k = \frac{3}{200}
$$

$$
k = 0.015
$$

Therefore:

$$
x = 0.015w
$$

### Worked Example:
It is given that $y$ varies directly as $x$. If $y = 0.14$ when $x = 0.2$, calculate $y$ when $x = 5$.

Since:

$$
y \propto x
$$

write:

$$
y = kx
$$

Substitute $y = 0.14$ and $x = 0.2$:

$$
0.14 = k(0.2)
$$

$$
k = 0.7
$$

So:

$$
y = 0.7x
$$

When $x = 5$:

$$
y = 0.7(5)
$$

$$
y = 3.5
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not use direct proportion without finding the correct power first.
- Keep the equation until the final substitution.
- If the question asks for $x$, rearrange the equation carefully.

## Concept: Direct Variation Using Proportion
The concept of proportion can also be used for direct variation.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
If:

$$
y \propto x^n
$$

then:

$$
\frac{y_1}{x_1^n} = \frac{y_2}{x_2^n}
$$

### Worked Example:
The area, $L$ cm$^2$, of a semicircle varies directly as the square of its diameter, $d$ cm. It is given that $L = 3.08$ when $d = 2.8$. Find $d$ when $L = 19.25$.

Since:

$$
L \propto d^2
$$

use:

$$
\frac{L_1}{d_1^2} = \frac{L_2}{d_2^2}
$$

Substitute:

$$
\frac{3.08}{2.8^2} = \frac{19.25}{d^2}
$$

Rearrange:

$$
d^2 = \frac{19.25 \times 2.8^2}{3.08}
$$

$$
d^2 = 49
$$

$$
d = 7
$$

Therefore:

$$
d = 7\text{ cm}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use the same power on both sides.
- If the relation is $L \propto d^2$, compare $\frac{L}{d^2}$.
- Do not compare $\frac{L}{d}$ unless $L \propto d$.

## Concept: Joint Variation
Joint variation is a direct variation where one variable varies as the product of two or more variables.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
If $y$ varies jointly as $x$ and $z$, then:

$$
y \propto xz
$$

The equation form is:

$$
y = kxz
$$

More generally:

$$
y \propto x^m z^n
$$

The equation form is:

$$
y = kx^m z^n
$$

where:

$$
m,n = 1,2,3,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}
$$

### Examples:
- $p$ varies directly as $q$ and $\sqrt{r}$:

$$
p \propto q\sqrt{r}
$$

$$
p = kq\sqrt{r}
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the square of $w$ and the cube of $x$:

$$
y \propto w^2x^3
$$

$$
y = kw^2x^3
$$

- The volume of a prism, $V$, varies directly as the area of cross-section, $A$, and height, $h$:

$$
V \propto Ah
$$

$$
V = kAh
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Joint variation is still a type of direct variation.
- “Varies directly as $x$ and $z$” means multiply $x$ and $z$.
- Do not add the variables.

## Concept: Solving Joint Variation Problems
Joint variation problems involve finding the constant $k$ using more than two variables.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
1. Write the variation relation.
2. Change it into equation form.
3. Substitute all given values.
4. Find $k$.
5. Use the equation to solve the required value.

### Worked Example:
Given that:

$$
x \propto y^2z
$$

Express $x$ in terms of $y$ and $z$ if $x = 6$ when $y = 3$ and $z = 5$.

Write:

$$
x = ky^2z
$$

Substitute:

$$
6 = k(3^2)(5)
$$

$$
6 = 45k
$$

$$
k = \frac{2}{15}
$$

Therefore:

$$
x = \frac{2}{15}y^2z
$$

### Worked Example:
The gravitational potential energy, $E$ Joules, of an object varies directly as its mass, $m$ kg, gravitational acceleration, $g$ m s$^{-2}$, and height, $h$ m. Given that $E = 197$ when $m = 4$, $g = 9.81$, and $h = 5$, write an equation relating $E$, $m$, $g$, and $h$.

Since:

$$
E \propto mgh
$$

write:

$$
E = kmgh
$$

Substitute:

$$
197 = k(4)(9.81)(5)
$$

$$
197 = 196.2k
$$

$$
k \approx 1
$$

Therefore:

$$
E = mgh
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Substitute every given variable value when finding $k$.
- In joint variation, the variables are multiplied.
- Include units in application questions when needed.

## Concept: Inverse Variation
Inverse variation describes a relationship where one variable increases when another variable decreases at the same rate, and vice versa.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $y$ varies inversely as $x$, then:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x}
$$

- The equation form is:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

where $k$ is a constant.

- The value of $k$ can be found using:

$$
k = xy
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- In inverse variation, the product $xy$ is constant.
- If $x$ increases, $y$ decreases.
- If $x$ decreases, $y$ increases.

## Concept: Change in Inverse Variation
In inverse variation, the quantities change in opposite directions.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $x$ is doubled, then $y$ is halved.
- If $x$ is halved, then $y$ is doubled.
- If $x$ increases by a certain factor, $y$ decreases by the same factor.
- If $x$ decreases by a certain factor, $y$ increases by the same factor.

### Worked Example:
A runner completes a $42$ km marathon. The average speed varies inversely as the time taken.

If the time taken increases from $4$ hours to $8$ hours, the time is doubled.

Therefore, the average speed is halved.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not say both quantities increase together.
- Inverse variation means opposite change.
- Check whether the context makes sense, such as more workers needing fewer days.

## Concept: Determining Inverse Variation from a Table
A table can be used to determine whether two variables have inverse variation.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- For $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$, calculate $xy$.
- If all values of $xy$ are the same, then $y$ varies inversely as $x$.
- For $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$, calculate $x^2y$.
- If all values of $x^2y$ are the same, then $y$ varies inversely as $x^2$.

### Worked Example:
Determine whether $t$ varies inversely as $x$ or $x^2$.

| Number of workers, $x$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Time needed, $t$ | $180$ | $120$ | $90$ | $72$ | $60$ |

Check $xt$:

$$
2(180) = 360
$$

$$
3(120) = 360
$$

$$
4(90) = 360
$$

Since $xt$ is constant:

$$
t \propto \frac{1}{x}
$$

Check $x^2t$:

$$
2^2(180) = 720
$$

$$
3^2(120) = 1080
$$

Since $x^2t$ is not constant, $t$ does not vary inversely as $x^2$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- For inverse variation, check multiplication, not division.
- For $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$, check $x^2y$.
- The constant $k$ is the product based on the inverse relation.

## Concept: Inverse Variation with Powers
A variable can vary inversely as a power or root of another variable.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
For inverse variation:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x^n}
$$

The equation form is:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x^n}
$$

where:

$$
n = 1,2,3,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}
$$

Examples:
- $y$ varies inversely as $x$:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x}
$$

$$
y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

- $y$ varies inversely as the square of $x$:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}
$$

$$
y = \frac{k}{x^2}
$$

- $y$ varies inversely as the square root of $x$:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}
$$

$$
y = \frac{k}{\sqrt{x}}
$$

- $y$ varies inversely as the cube root of $x$:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x}}
$$

$$
y = \frac{k}{\sqrt[3]{x}}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ is only true when $n = 1$.
- If $y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}$, then $x^2y$ is constant.
- If $y \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}$, then $y\sqrt{x}$ is constant.

## Concept: Graph of Inverse Variation
The graph of inverse variation depends on what is plotted.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$, the graph of $y$ against $x$ is a hyperbola.
- The graph of $y$ against $\frac{1}{x}$ is a straight line through the origin.
- If $y \propto \frac{1}{x^n}$, the graph of $y$ against $\frac{1}{x^n}$ is a straight line through the origin.
- The gradient of this straight line is the constant $k$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A hyperbola on the graph of $y$ against $x$ can indicate inverse variation.
- To test inverse variation using a straight line, plot $y$ against $\frac{1}{x^n}$.
- The variable $x$ cannot be $0$ in inverse variation.

## Concept: Expressing an Inverse Variation Equation
To express one variable in terms of another in inverse variation, use the equation form and find $k$.

### Worked Example:
It is given that $y = 3$ when $x = 7$. Express $y$ in terms of $x$ if $y$ varies inversely as $x$.

Since:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x}
$$

write:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

Substitute:

$$
3 = \frac{k}{7}
$$

$$
k = 21
$$

Therefore:

$$
y = \frac{21}{x}
$$

### Worked Example:
It is given that $y = 3$ when $x = 7$. Express $y$ in terms of $x$ if $y$ varies inversely as $x^2$.

Since:

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x^2}
$$

write:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x^2}
$$

Substitute:

$$
3 = \frac{k}{7^2}
$$

$$
k = 147
$$

Therefore:

$$
y = \frac{147}{x^2}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not write $y = kx$ for inverse variation.
- Multiply by the denominator to find $k$.
- Check whether the inverse relation involves $x$, $x^2$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{x}$, or $\sqrt[3]{x}$.

## Concept: Solving Inverse Variation Problems
Inverse variation problems usually involve quantities such as time and number of workers, pressure and volume, or force and distance.

### Worked Example:
The gas pressure, $p$, of a mass of gas varies inversely as the volume, $V$, if the temperature is fixed. When $V = 80$ cm$^3$, $p = 190.25$ kPa. Find $V$ when $p = 121.76$ kPa.

Since:

$$
p \propto \frac{1}{V}
$$

write:

$$
p = \frac{k}{V}
$$

Substitute:

$$
190.25 = \frac{k}{80}
$$

$$
k = 190.25 \times 80
$$

$$
k = 15220
$$

So:

$$
p = \frac{15220}{V}
$$

When $p = 121.76$:

$$
121.76 = \frac{15220}{V}
$$

$$
V = \frac{15220}{121.76}
$$

$$
V = 125
$$

Therefore:

$$
V = 125\text{ cm}^3
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Inverse variation often appears when one quantity must be shared or balanced.
- More workers usually means less time.
- Larger volume usually means lower gas pressure if temperature is fixed.

## Concept: Combined Variation
Combined variation involves a combination of direct variation or joint variation with inverse variation.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
For combined variation, if $y$ varies directly as $x^m$ and inversely as $z^n$, then:

$$
y \propto \frac{x^m}{z^n}
$$

The equation form is:

$$
y = \frac{kx^m}{z^n}
$$

where:

$$
m,n = 1,2,3,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3}
$$

### Examples:
- $y$ varies directly as $x$ and inversely as $z$:

$$
y \propto \frac{x}{z}
$$

$$
y = \frac{kx}{z}
$$

- $y$ varies directly as the square of $x$ and inversely as the square root of $z$:

$$
y \propto \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{z}}
$$

$$
y = \frac{kx^2}{\sqrt{z}}
$$

- $y$ varies directly as $w$ and the cube of $x$, and inversely as the cube root of $z$:

$$
y \propto \frac{wx^3}{\sqrt[3]{z}}
$$

$$
y = \frac{kwx^3}{\sqrt[3]{z}}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Direct variables are placed in the numerator.
- Inverse variables are placed in the denominator.
- Joint variation variables in the direct part are multiplied together.

## Concept: Solving Combined Variation Problems
Combined variation problems require identifying both direct and inverse parts.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
1. Identify the variable that is being described.
2. Put directly proportional variables in the numerator.
3. Put inversely proportional variables in the denominator.
4. Insert $k$.
5. Substitute given values to find $k$.
6. Use the equation to solve the unknown.

### Worked Example:
It is given that $y$ varies directly as the square of $x$ and inversely as the square root of $z$. If $y = 8$ when $x = 4$ and $z = 36$, express $y$ in terms of $x$ and $z$.

Since:

$$
y \propto \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{z}}
$$

write:

$$
y = \frac{kx^2}{\sqrt{z}}
$$

Substitute:

$$
8 = \frac{k(4^2)}{\sqrt{36}}
$$

$$
8 = \frac{16k}{6}
$$

$$
48 = 16k
$$

$$
k = 3
$$

Therefore:

$$
y = \frac{3x^2}{\sqrt{z}}
$$

### Worked Example:
The pressure, $p$ N m$^{-2}$, on the tyre of a wheelbarrow varies directly as the mass, $m$ kg, and inversely as the surface area, $l$ m$^2$, of the tyre touching the ground. Given that $p = 45000$ when $m = 90$ and $l = 0.02$, find $p$ when $m = 120$ and $l = 0.5$.

Since:

$$
p \propto \frac{m}{l}
$$

write:

$$
p = \frac{km}{l}
$$

Substitute:

$$
45000 = \frac{k(90)}{0.02}
$$

$$
k = \frac{45000(0.02)}{90}
$$

$$
k = 10
$$

So:

$$
p = \frac{10m}{l}
$$

When $m = 120$ and $l = 0.5$:

$$
p = \frac{10(120)}{0.5}
$$

$$
p = 2400
$$

Therefore:

$$
p = 2400\text{ N m}^{-2}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- If the inverse quantity increases, the dependent quantity decreases.
- If the direct quantity increases, the dependent quantity increases.
- Always check which variable is fixed when explaining changes.

## Concept: Variation in Word Problems
Variation is commonly used to model real-life mathematical and scientific relationships.

**Examples of Applications:**
- Distance varies directly as speed and time:

$$
s \propto vt
$$

- Simple interest varies directly as principal, rate, and time:

$$
I \propto Prt
$$

- Volume of a prism varies directly as cross-sectional area and height:

$$
V \propto Ah
$$

- Pressure can vary directly as force and inversely as area:

$$
p \propto \frac{F}{A}
$$

- Gas pressure varies inversely as volume when temperature is fixed:

$$
p \propto \frac{1}{V}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Translate the sentence carefully before forming the equation.
- “Varies directly as” means multiply in the numerator.
- “Varies inversely as” means place in the denominator.
- “Varies jointly as” means multiply the direct variables together.

## Concept: Solving Variation Problems Using a Strategy
Variation problems can be solved systematically.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
1. Understand the problem.
2. Identify the type of variation.
3. Write the variation relation.
4. Convert it into equation form.
5. Substitute given values to find $k$.
6. Substitute required values.
7. Write the final answer with units.
8. Explain the answer in context if required.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not skip the equation form.
- Do not assume $k = 1$ unless proven.
- Use the correct units in the final answer.
- For context questions, explain whether the quantity increases, decreases, or remains suitable.

## Concept: Percentage Change in Variation
Variation can be used to determine percentage changes.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- In direct variation, if one variable changes by a factor, the dependent variable changes by the same factor.
- In joint variation, multiply the change factors.
- In inverse variation, divide by the change factor.
- In combined variation, multiply the direct change factors and divide by the inverse change factors.

### Worked Example:
The area of a triangle, $A$, varies directly as its base, $x$, and height, $y$.

If the base increases by $20\%$ and the height decreases by $10\%$, find the percentage change in area.

Since:

$$
A \propto xy
$$

New base factor:

$$
1.20
$$

New height factor:

$$
0.90
$$

New area factor:

$$
1.20 \times 0.90 = 1.08
$$

Therefore, the area increases by:

$$
8\%
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Increase by $20\%$ means multiply by $1.20$.
- Decrease by $10\%$ means multiply by $0.90$.
- If the final factor is greater than $1$, there is an increase.
- If the final factor is less than $1$, there is a decrease.

## Concept: Summary of Key Variation Forms
The main variation forms in this topic are:

### Direct Variation

$$
y \propto x^n
$$

$$
y = kx^n
$$

$$
k = \frac{y}{x^n}
$$

### Inverse Variation

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x^n}
$$

$$
y = \frac{k}{x^n}
$$

$$
k = yx^n
$$

### Joint Variation

$$
y \propto x^m z^n
$$

$$
y = kx^m z^n
$$

$$
k = \frac{y}{x^m z^n}
$$

### Combined Variation

$$
y \propto \frac{x^m}{z^n}
$$

$$
y = \frac{kx^m}{z^n}
$$

$$
k = \frac{yz^n}{x^m}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Direct variation uses multiplication by $x^n$.
- Inverse variation uses division by $x^n$.
- Joint variation multiplies two or more direct variables.
- Combined variation combines direct and inverse relationships.
- Always find $k$ before solving for unknown values.
