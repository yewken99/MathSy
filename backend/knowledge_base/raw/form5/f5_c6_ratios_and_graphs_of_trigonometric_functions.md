## Overview
This chapter covers the values of sine, cosine and tangent for angles between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, the use of corresponding reference angles, unit circle relationships, signs of trigonometric ratios in different quadrants, and the graphs of sine, cosine and tangent functions. Students also learn how changes in constants affect the amplitude, period and vertical position of trigonometric graphs.

## Concept: Unit Circle
A unit circle is a circle with radius $1$ unit and centre at the origin $O$.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- The unit circle is drawn on the Cartesian plane.
- The $x$-axis and $y$-axis divide the circle into four quadrants.
- An angle $\theta$ is measured from the positive $x$-axis in the anticlockwise direction.
- A point $P(x,y)$ on the unit circle can be used to find $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

### Quadrants:
| Quadrant | Angle Range |
|---|---|
| Quadrant I | $0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$ |
| Quadrant II | $90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$ |
| Quadrant III | $180^\circ < \theta < 270^\circ$ |
| Quadrant IV | $270^\circ < \theta < 360^\circ$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The angle starts from the positive $x$-axis.
- The anticlockwise direction is the positive direction.
- Always identify the quadrant before deciding the sign of $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ or $\tan\theta$.

## Concept: Sine, Cosine and Tangent on the Unit Circle
For a point $P(x,y)$ on the unit circle:

$$
\sin\theta = y
$$

$$
\cos\theta = x
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- $\sin\theta$ uses the $y$-coordinate.
- $\cos\theta$ uses the $x$-coordinate.
- $\tan\theta$ uses $\frac{y}{x}$.
- $\tan\theta$ is undefined when $x=0$ because division by zero is not possible.

## Concept: Signs of Sine, Cosine and Tangent in Quadrants
The sign of each trigonometric ratio depends on the quadrant.

| Quadrant | $\sin\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\tan\theta$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| I | $+$ | $+$ | $+$ |
| II | $+$ | $-$ | $-$ |
| III | $-$ | $-$ | $+$ |
| IV | $-$ | $+$ | $-$ |

### Easy Memory:
- Quadrant I: All positive.
- Quadrant II: Sine positive.
- Quadrant III: Tangent positive.
- Quadrant IV: Cosine positive.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not decide the sign based only on the reference angle.
- First identify the quadrant, then apply the sign.
- A useful memory method is **ASTC**:
  - A = All positive.
  - S = Sine positive.
  - T = Tangent positive.
  - C = Cosine positive.

## Concept: Corresponding Reference Angle
The corresponding reference angle, $\alpha$, is the acute angle between the terminal arm of $\theta$ and the $x$-axis.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- The reference angle is always less than $90^\circ$.
- For Quadrant I, the angle itself is the reference angle.
- For Quadrants II, III and IV, use the correct formula.

### Reference Angle Formula:
| Quadrant | Formula |
|---|---|
| I | $\alpha = \theta$ |
| II | $\alpha = 180^\circ - \theta$ |
| III | $\alpha = \theta - 180^\circ$ |
| IV | $\alpha = 360^\circ - \theta$ |

### Worked Example:
Find the corresponding reference angle for $138^\circ$.

Since:

$$
90^\circ < 138^\circ < 180^\circ
$$

the angle is in Quadrant II.

$$
\alpha = 180^\circ - 138^\circ
$$

$$
\alpha = 42^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
Find the corresponding reference angle for $239^\circ$.

Since:

$$
180^\circ < 239^\circ < 270^\circ
$$

the angle is in Quadrant III.

$$
\alpha = 239^\circ - 180^\circ
$$

$$
\alpha = 59^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
Find the corresponding reference angle for $312^\circ$.

Since:

$$
270^\circ < 312^\circ < 360^\circ
$$

the angle is in Quadrant IV.

$$
\alpha = 360^\circ - 312^\circ
$$

$$
\alpha = 48^\circ
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The reference angle must be acute.
- For Quadrant II, subtract from $180^\circ$.
- For Quadrant III, subtract $180^\circ$ from the angle.
- For Quadrant IV, subtract the angle from $360^\circ$.

## Concept: Relationship Between Trigonometric Values and Reference Angle
The value of a trigonometric function can be expressed using its corresponding reference angle.

### Quadrant II:
In Quadrant II:

$$
\alpha = 180^\circ - \theta
$$

$$
\sin\theta = +\sin\alpha
$$

$$
\cos\theta = -\cos\alpha
$$

$$
\tan\theta = -\tan\alpha
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sin\theta = \sin(180^\circ-\theta)
$$

$$
\cos\theta = -\cos(180^\circ-\theta)
$$

$$
\tan\theta = -\tan(180^\circ-\theta)
$$

### Quadrant III:
In Quadrant III:

$$
\alpha = \theta - 180^\circ
$$

$$
\sin\theta = -\sin\alpha
$$

$$
\cos\theta = -\cos\alpha
$$

$$
\tan\theta = +\tan\alpha
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sin\theta = -\sin(\theta-180^\circ)
$$

$$
\cos\theta = -\cos(\theta-180^\circ)
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \tan(\theta-180^\circ)
$$

### Quadrant IV:
In Quadrant IV:

$$
\alpha = 360^\circ - \theta
$$

$$
\sin\theta = -\sin\alpha
$$

$$
\cos\theta = +\cos\alpha
$$

$$
\tan\theta = -\tan\alpha
$$

Therefore:

$$
\sin\theta = -\sin(360^\circ-\theta)
$$

$$
\cos\theta = \cos(360^\circ-\theta)
$$

$$
\tan\theta = -\tan(360^\circ-\theta)
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not forget the positive or negative sign.
- Use the quadrant to determine the sign.
- The reference angle is used to find the basic value.
- The quadrant determines whether the final answer is positive or negative.

## Concept: Stating Trigonometric Functions Using Reference Angles
Sometimes the question asks for the relationship only, not the final decimal value.

### Worked Example:
State the relationship for:

$$
\sin 167^\circ
$$

Since $167^\circ$ is in Quadrant II, sine is positive.

$$
\sin 167^\circ = \sin(180^\circ - 167^\circ)
$$

$$
\sin 167^\circ = \sin 13^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
State the relationship for:

$$
\cos 258^\circ
$$

Since $258^\circ$ is in Quadrant III, cosine is negative.

$$
\cos 258^\circ = -\cos(258^\circ - 180^\circ)
$$

$$
\cos 258^\circ = -\cos 78^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
State the relationship for:

$$
\tan 349^\circ
$$

Since $349^\circ$ is in Quadrant IV, tangent is negative.

$$
\tan 349^\circ = -\tan(360^\circ - 349^\circ)
$$

$$
\tan 349^\circ = -\tan 11^\circ
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not calculate too early if the question only asks for the relationship.
- Show the quadrant sign clearly.
- Write the reference angle correctly.

## Concept: Finding Trigonometric Values Using Reference Angles
To find the value of $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ or $\tan\theta$:

1. Identify the quadrant.
2. Find the reference angle.
3. Decide the sign.
4. Calculate the value using the reference angle.
5. Apply the correct sign.

### Worked Example:
Find:

$$
\sin 104^\circ
$$

Since $104^\circ$ is in Quadrant II, sine is positive.

Reference angle:

$$
180^\circ - 104^\circ = 76^\circ
$$

So:

$$
\sin 104^\circ = \sin 76^\circ
$$

$$
= 0.9703
$$

### Worked Example:
Find:

$$
\cos 208.2^\circ
$$

Since $208.2^\circ$ is in Quadrant III, cosine is negative.

Reference angle:

$$
208.2^\circ - 180^\circ = 28.2^\circ
$$

So:

$$
\cos 208.2^\circ = -\cos 28.2^\circ
$$

$$
= -0.8813
$$

### Worked Example:
Find:

$$
\tan 318^\circ17'
$$

Since $318^\circ17'$ is in Quadrant IV, tangent is negative.

Reference angle:

$$
360^\circ - 318^\circ17' = 41^\circ43'
$$

So:

$$
\tan 318^\circ17' = -\tan 41^\circ43'
$$

$$
= -0.8915
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Make sure the calculator is in degree mode.
- For minutes, use the degree-minute-second function correctly.
- The sign must follow the quadrant, not the calculator display only.

## Concept: Exact Values for $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$
Some angles can be solved without a calculator.

| Angle | $\sin\theta$ | $\cos\theta$ | $\tan\theta$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $30^\circ$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ |
| $45^\circ$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ | $1$ |
| $60^\circ$ | $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $\sqrt{3}$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Remember that $\sin 30^\circ = \cos 60^\circ$.
- Remember that $\cos 30^\circ = \sin 60^\circ$.
- $\tan 45^\circ = 1$.
- Exact answers should usually be left in surd form.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:

## Concept: Finding Angle When Trigonometric Value Is Given
When a trigonometric value is given, find all possible angles between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.

### Method:
1. Identify the sign of the trigonometric value.
2. Identify the possible quadrants.
3. Find the reference angle using inverse trigonometric function.
4. Use the quadrant to find all possible values of $\theta$.

### Quadrant Possibilities:
| Given Function Sign | Possible Quadrants |
|---|---|
| $\sin\theta$ positive | I and II |
| $\sin\theta$ negative | III and IV |
| $\cos\theta$ positive | I and IV |
| $\cos\theta$ negative | II and III |
| $\tan\theta$ positive | I and III |
| $\tan\theta$ negative | II and IV |

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
\sin\theta = 0.6157
$$

Since sine is positive, $\theta$ is in Quadrant I or II.

Reference angle:

$$
\alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.6157)
$$

$$
\alpha = 38^\circ
$$

So:

$$
\theta = 38^\circ
$$

or:

$$
\theta = 180^\circ - 38^\circ
$$

$$
= 142^\circ
$$

Therefore:

$$
\theta = 38^\circ \text{ or } 142^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
\cos\theta = -0.4226
$$

Since cosine is negative, $\theta$ is in Quadrant II or III.

Reference angle:

$$
\alpha = \cos^{-1}(0.4226)
$$

$$
\alpha = 65^\circ
$$

So:

$$
\theta = 180^\circ - 65^\circ
$$

$$
= 115^\circ
$$

or:

$$
\theta = 180^\circ + 65^\circ
$$

$$
= 245^\circ
$$

Therefore:

$$
\theta = 115^\circ \text{ or } 245^\circ
$$

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
\tan\theta = -1.4826
$$

Since tangent is negative, $\theta$ is in Quadrant II or IV.

Reference angle:

$$
\alpha = \tan^{-1}(1.4826)
$$

$$
\alpha = 56^\circ
$$

So:

$$
\theta = 180^\circ - 56^\circ
$$

$$
= 124^\circ
$$

or:

$$
\theta = 360^\circ - 56^\circ
$$

$$
= 304^\circ
$$

Therefore:

$$
\theta = 124^\circ \text{ or } 304^\circ
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- When using inverse trigonometric functions, use the positive value to find the reference angle.
- The negative sign only tells the quadrant.
- Usually there are two answers between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
- Round according to the question instruction.

## Concept: Solving Problems Involving Sine, Cosine and Tangent
Trigonometric ratios can be used in problems involving geometry, movement and real-life contexts.

### General Method:
1. Identify the quadrant.
2. Identify the reference angle.
3. Decide whether the value is positive or negative.
4. Use the correct ratio:
   - $\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
   - $\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
   - $\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$
5. State the answer with units if needed.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Check whether the angle is in Quadrant I, II, III or IV.
- For real-life length questions, distance is usually positive.
- For coordinate or direction questions, the sign may be important.
- Draw or label the triangle clearly.

## Concept: Graph of $y=\sin x$
The sine graph is a smooth periodic curve.

### Key Characteristics:
For $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$:

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Maximum value | $1$ |
| Occurs when | $x=90^\circ$ |
| Minimum value | $-1$ |
| Occurs when | $x=270^\circ$ |
| $x$-intercepts | $0^\circ, 180^\circ, 360^\circ$ |
| $y$-intercept | $0$ |
| Period | $360^\circ$ |

### Important Points:
| $x$ | $0^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | $270^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $y=\sin x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $0$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The sine graph starts at $0$ when $x=0^\circ$.
- It reaches maximum at $90^\circ$.
- It crosses the $x$-axis again at $180^\circ$.
- It reaches minimum at $270^\circ$.

## Concept: Graph of $y=\cos x$
The cosine graph is also a smooth periodic curve.

### Key Characteristics:
For $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$:

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Maximum value | $1$ |
| Occurs when | $x=0^\circ$ and $x=360^\circ$ |
| Minimum value | $-1$ |
| Occurs when | $x=180^\circ$ |
| $x$-intercepts | $90^\circ, 270^\circ$ |
| $y$-intercept | $1$ |
| Period | $360^\circ$ |

### Important Points:
| $x$ | $0^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | $270^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $y=\cos x$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The cosine graph starts at $1$ when $x=0^\circ$.
- It reaches minimum at $180^\circ$.
- It returns to $1$ at $360^\circ$.
- Do not confuse sine and cosine starting points.

## Concept: Graph of $y=\tan x$
The tangent graph is different from sine and cosine.

### Key Characteristics:
For $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$:

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Maximum value | No maximum |
| Minimum value | No minimum |
| $x$-intercepts | $0^\circ, 180^\circ, 360^\circ$ |
| $y$-intercept | $0$ |
| Undefined values | $90^\circ, 270^\circ$ |
| Period | $180^\circ$ |

### Important Points:
| $x$ | $0^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | $270^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $y=\tan x$ | $0$ | undefined | $0$ | undefined | $0$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tangent has no maximum or minimum value.
- $\tan 90^\circ$ and $\tan 270^\circ$ are undefined.
- Tangent graph has vertical asymptotes at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$.
- The tangent graph repeats every $180^\circ$.

## Concept: Period of Trigonometric Graphs
The period is the interval of one complete cycle.

### Basic Periods:
| Function | Period |
|---|---:|
| $y=\sin x$ | $360^\circ$ |
| $y=\cos x$ | $360^\circ$ |
| $y=\tan x$ | $180^\circ$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Sine and cosine have period $360^\circ$.
- Tangent has period $180^\circ$.
- The period changes when the function has $bx$.

## Concept: Amplitude
Amplitude is the maximum distance from the centre line of the graph.

### Formula:
$$
\text{Amplitude} =
\frac{\text{maximum value} - \text{minimum value}}{2}
$$

For:

$$
y = a\sin bx + c
$$

and:

$$
y = a\cos bx + c
$$

the amplitude is:

$$
a
$$

when $a>0$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tangent graphs do not have amplitude.
- Amplitude is not the maximum value if the graph is shifted up or down.
- For sine and cosine, amplitude is controlled by $a$.

## Concept: Graph of $y=a\sin bx+c$
For:

$$
y = a\sin bx + c
$$

where $a>0$ and $b>0$:

### Effects:
| Constant | Effect |
|---|---|
| $a$ | Changes amplitude |
| $b$ | Changes period |
| $c$ | Moves graph vertically |

### Amplitude:
$$
\text{Amplitude} = a
$$

### Period:
$$
\text{Period} = \frac{360^\circ}{b}
$$

### Vertical Shift:
- If $c>0$, graph moves $c$ units up.
- If $c<0$, graph moves $c$ units down.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- $a$ changes height.
- $b$ changes horizontal compression or expansion.
- $c$ changes vertical position.
- Use $360^\circ$ for sine period.

## Concept: Graph of $y=a\cos bx+c$
For:

$$
y = a\cos bx + c
$$

where $a>0$ and $b>0$:

### Effects:
| Constant | Effect |
|---|---|
| $a$ | Changes amplitude |
| $b$ | Changes period |
| $c$ | Moves graph vertically |

### Amplitude:
$$
\text{Amplitude} = a
$$

### Period:
$$
\text{Period} = \frac{360^\circ}{b}
$$

### Vertical Shift:
- If $c>0$, graph moves $c$ units up.
- If $c<0$, graph moves $c$ units down.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Cosine starts at its maximum when there is no shift.
- Use the centre line $y=c$ when sketching.
- Use $360^\circ$ for cosine period.

## Concept: Graph of $y=a\tan bx+c$
For:

$$
y = a\tan bx + c
$$

where $a>0$ and $b>0$:

### Effects:
| Constant | Effect |
|---|---|
| $a$ | Changes graph steepness or curvature |
| $b$ | Changes period |
| $c$ | Moves graph vertically |

### Period:
$$
\text{Period} = \frac{180^\circ}{b}
$$

### Vertical Shift:
- If $c>0$, graph moves $c$ units up.
- If $c<0$, graph moves $c$ units down.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tangent has no amplitude.
- Use $180^\circ$ for tangent period.
- Tangent has undefined values where vertical asymptotes occur.

## Concept: Effect of Changing $a$
The constant $a$ changes the vertical stretch of the graph.

### For Sine and Cosine:
If:

$$
y = a\sin x
$$

or:

$$
y = a\cos x
$$

then the amplitude becomes:

$$
a
$$

### Example:
For:

$$
y = 2\sin x
$$

the amplitude is:

$$
2
$$

The maximum value is:

$$
2
$$

and the minimum value is:

$$
-2
$$

### For Tangent:
For tangent functions, changing $a$ changes the steepness or curvature, but there is no amplitude.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not say tangent has amplitude.
- For sine and cosine, $a$ affects maximum and minimum values.
- If $c=0$, maximum is $a$ and minimum is $-a$.

## Concept: Effect of Changing $b$
The constant $b$ changes the period.

### For Sine and Cosine:
$$
\text{Period} = \frac{360^\circ}{b}
$$

### For Tangent:
$$
\text{Period} = \frac{180^\circ}{b}
$$

### Example:
For:

$$
y = \sin 2x
$$

the period is:

$$
\frac{360^\circ}{2}
$$

$$
= 180^\circ
$$

So the graph completes one cycle in $180^\circ$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Bigger $b$ means shorter period.
- Smaller $b$ means longer period.
- Use the correct formula for tangent.

## Concept: Effect of Changing $c$
The constant $c$ moves the graph up or down.

### Rules:
If:

$$
c>0
$$

the graph moves $c$ units up.

If:

$$
c<0
$$

the graph moves $c$ units down.

### Example:
For:

$$
y = \sin x + 1
$$

the graph of $y=\sin x$ moves $1$ unit up.

The centre line becomes:

$$
y=1
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- $c$ does not change the period.
- $c$ does not change the amplitude.
- $c$ changes the centre line.

## Concept: Sketching Transformed Sine Graphs
To sketch sine graphs such as:

$$
y = a\sin bx + c
$$

follow these steps:

1. Find the amplitude.
2. Find the period.
3. Find the centre line.
4. Plot key points.
5. Draw the smooth sine curve.

### Key Sine Shape:
For one basic cycle:

$$
0 \rightarrow 1 \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow -1 \rightarrow 0
$$

### Worked Example:
Sketch:

$$
y = 2\sin 2x
$$

Amplitude:

$$
2
$$

Period:

$$
\frac{360^\circ}{2} = 180^\circ
$$

There is no vertical shift, so the centre line is:

$$
y=0
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The graph must complete one cycle over the period.
- Use the amplitude for maximum and minimum values.
- For $y=2\sin 2x$, the graph is taller and horizontally compressed.

## Concept: Sketching Transformed Cosine Graphs
To sketch cosine graphs such as:

$$
y = a\cos bx + c
$$

follow the same idea.

### Key Cosine Shape:
For one basic cycle:

$$
1 \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow -1 \rightarrow 0 \rightarrow 1
$$

### Worked Example:
Sketch:

$$
y = 2\cos x + 1
$$

Amplitude:

$$
2
$$

Period:

$$
360^\circ
$$

Centre line:

$$
y=1
$$

Maximum value:

$$
1+2=3
$$

Minimum value:

$$
1-2=-1
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Cosine starts at the maximum value if there is no phase shift.
- Add $c$ to the maximum and minimum values.
- The centre line is $y=c$.

## Concept: Sketching Transformed Tangent Graphs
To sketch tangent graphs such as:

$$
y = a\tan bx + c
$$

identify the period and vertical shift.

### Worked Example:
Sketch:

$$
y = \tan 0.5x - 1
$$

Period:

$$
\frac{180^\circ}{0.5}
$$

$$
= 360^\circ
$$

Vertical shift:

$$
-1
$$

So the graph moves $1$ unit down.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Tangent has no amplitude.
- Watch the undefined values.
- The graph has vertical asymptotes.
- The value of $c$ shifts the whole graph up or down.

## Concept: Modelling Real-Life Situations with Trigonometric Graphs
Trigonometric graphs can model periodic situations such as breathing cycles, tides, waves and rotating objects.

### General Sine Model:
$$
y = a\sin bx + c
$$

### General Cosine Model:
$$
y = a\cos bx + c
$$

### Steps to Build a Model:
1. Find the amplitude.
2. Find the period.
3. Find $b$ using the period formula.
4. Find the centre line $c$.
5. Choose sine or cosine based on the starting point.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Amplitude is half of the total height.
- Centre line is the average of maximum and minimum values.
- Period is the time for one full cycle.
- Use sine if the graph starts at the centre line.
- Use cosine if the graph starts at a maximum or minimum.

## Concept: Finding Amplitude from a Graph
If the maximum and minimum values are known:

$$
\text{Amplitude} =
\frac{\text{maximum value} - \text{minimum value}}{2}
$$

### Example:
If maximum value is $4.0$ and minimum value is $1.8$:

$$
\text{Amplitude} = \frac{4.0 - 1.8}{2}
$$

$$
= 1.1
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not subtract from zero unless the centre line is zero.
- Always use maximum minus minimum, then divide by $2$.
- Amplitude is always positive.

## Concept: Finding Centre Line from a Graph
The centre line is halfway between the maximum and minimum values.

### Formula:
$$
c =
\frac{\text{maximum value} + \text{minimum value}}{2}
$$

### Example:
If maximum value is $4.0$ and minimum value is $1.8$:

$$
c = \frac{4.0 + 1.8}{2}
$$

$$
= 2.9
$$

So the centre line is:

$$
y = 2.9
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The centre line is not always the $x$-axis.
- If the graph is shifted upward, $c$ is positive.
- If the graph is shifted downward, $c$ is negative.

## Concept: Finding $b$ from Period
Use the period to find $b$.

### For Sine and Cosine:
$$
b = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{period}}
$$

### For Tangent:
$$
b = \frac{180^\circ}{\text{period}}
$$

### Worked Example:
A respiratory cycle repeats every $5$ seconds.

For sine:

$$
\frac{360^\circ}{b} = 5
$$

So:

$$
b = \frac{360^\circ}{5}
$$

$$
b = 72
$$

If amplitude is $0.6$ and centre line is $0$, then the model is:

$$
y = 0.6\sin 72x
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use $360^\circ$ for sine and cosine.
- Use $180^\circ$ for tangent.
- Period must match the unit of $x$ in the question.

## Concept: Summary of Key Rules

### Unit Circle
$$
\sin\theta = y
$$

$$
\cos\theta = x
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{y}{x}
$$

### Reference Angles
| Quadrant | Reference Angle |
|---|---|
| I | $\alpha=\theta$ |
| II | $\alpha=180^\circ-\theta$ |
| III | $\alpha=\theta-180^\circ$ |
| IV | $\alpha=360^\circ-\theta$ |

### Signs of Trigonometric Ratios
| Quadrant | Positive Ratio |
|---|---|
| I | All |
| II | Sine |
| III | Tangent |
| IV | Cosine |

### Basic Graph Characteristics
| Function | Maximum | Minimum | $x$-intercepts | $y$-intercept | Period |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| $y=\sin x$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $0^\circ,180^\circ,360^\circ$ | $0$ | $360^\circ$ |
| $y=\cos x$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $90^\circ,270^\circ$ | $1$ | $360^\circ$ |
| $y=\tan x$ | none | none | $0^\circ,180^\circ,360^\circ$ | $0$ | $180^\circ$ |

### Amplitude
For sine and cosine:

$$
\text{Amplitude} = a
$$

For tangent:

$$
\text{No amplitude}
$$

### Period
For sine and cosine:

$$
\text{Period} = \frac{360^\circ}{b}
$$

For tangent:

$$
\text{Period} = \frac{180^\circ}{b}
$$

### Vertical Shift
For:

$$
y = a\sin bx + c
$$

$$
y = a\cos bx + c
$$

$$
y = a\tan bx + c
$$

the graph moves:
- $c$ units up if $c>0$.
- $c$ units down if $c<0$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Identify the quadrant before deciding the sign.
- Reference angle is always acute.
- Use exact values for $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ when calculators are not allowed.
- Sine and cosine have amplitude; tangent does not.
- Sine and cosine period uses $360^\circ$.
- Tangent period uses $180^\circ$.
- $\tan 90^\circ$ and $\tan 270^\circ$ are undefined.
- For graph modelling, amplitude comes from half the difference between maximum and minimum values.
