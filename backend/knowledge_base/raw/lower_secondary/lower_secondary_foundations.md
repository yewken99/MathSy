# Lower Secondary Foundations
**Level:** Form 1 to Form 3 Foundation  
**Purpose:** Bridge guide for students who forgot important lower secondary Mathematics basics before learning SPM Mathematics topics.

## Overview
This guide reviews the most important lower secondary foundations that students often need before solving Form 4 and Form 5 Mathematics questions. It focuses on algebraic expansion, factorisation, basic linear equations, basic laws of indices, and polygon geometry.

Use this guide when you feel stuck because the SPM question uses earlier skills such as:
- expanding brackets,
- factorising algebraic expressions,
- solving simple equations,
- simplifying powers and indices,
- finding angles in triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons.

---

# 1. Algebraic Expressions

## Concept: Algebraic Term
An algebraic term is a number, a variable, or a product of numbers and variables.

Examples:

\[
3x,\quad -5y,\quad 7ab,\quad \frac{1}{2}p
\]

In the term:

\[
3x
\]

- \(3\) is the coefficient,
- \(x\) is the variable.

### Common Mistakes
- In \(-4x\), the coefficient is \(-4\), not \(4\).
- In \(x\), the coefficient is \(1\).
- In \(-x\), the coefficient is \(-1\).

---

## Concept: Like Terms
Like terms have the same variables with the same powers.

Examples of like terms:

\[
3x,\ 7x,\ -2x
\]

\[
5a^2,\ -8a^2,\ a^2
\]

Examples of unlike terms:

\[
3x \text{ and } 3y
\]

\[
2a \text{ and } 2a^2
\]

### Key Rule
Only like terms can be added or subtracted.

### Worked Example

Simplify:

\[
5x+3x-2x
\]

\[
=(5+3-2)x
\]

\[
=6x
\]

### Worked Example

Simplify:

\[
4a+3b-2a+5b
\]

Group like terms:

\[
4a-2a+3b+5b
\]

\[
=2a+8b
\]

### Common Mistakes
- \(3x+2y\) cannot become \(5xy\).
- \(4a+3a^2\) cannot become \(7a^3\).
- Always group like terms before simplifying.

---

# 2. Algebraic Expansion

## Concept: Expansion
Expansion means removing brackets by multiplying each term inside the bracket.

### Key Rule

\[
a(b+c)=ab+ac
\]

\[
a(b-c)=ab-ac
\]

This is called the distributive law.

---

## Expanding One Bracket

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
3(x+4)
\]

Multiply \(3\) with every term inside the bracket:

\[
3(x)+3(4)
\]

\[
=3x+12
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
-2(5x-3)
\]

\[
=-2(5x)+(-2)(-3)
\]

\[
=-10x+6
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Do not multiply only the first term.
- Be careful when a negative number is outside the bracket.
- \(-2(5x-3)\) becomes \(-10x+6\), not \(-10x-6\).

---

## Expanding Two Brackets

### Key Rule

\[
(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd
\]

Each term in the first bracket must multiply each term in the second bracket.

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(x+3)(x+5)
\]

\[
=x(x)+x(5)+3(x)+3(5)
\]

\[
=x^2+5x+3x+15
\]

\[
=x^2+8x+15
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(x-4)(x+2)
\]

\[
=x(x)+x(2)-4(x)-4(2)
\]

\[
=x^2+2x-4x-8
\]

\[
=x^2-2x-8
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(2x+3)(x-5)
\]

\[
=2x(x)+2x(-5)+3(x)+3(-5)
\]

\[
=2x^2-10x+3x-15
\]

\[
=2x^2-7x-15
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Do not forget to multiply all four pairs.
- Watch the signs carefully.
- Combine like terms after expanding.

---

## Special Expansion: Perfect Square

### Formula 1

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
\]

### Formula 2

\[
(a-b)^2=a^2-2ab+b^2
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(x+4)^2
\]

\[
=(x+4)(x+4)
\]

\[
=x^2+4x+4x+16
\]

\[
=x^2+8x+16
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(2x-3)^2
\]

\[
=(2x)^2-2(2x)(3)+3^2
\]

\[
=4x^2-12x+9
\]

### Common Mistakes
- \((x+4)^2\) is not \(x^2+16\).
- The middle term is important.
- \((a-b)^2\) still has \(+b^2\) at the end.

---

## Special Expansion: Difference of Two Squares

### Formula

\[
(a+b)(a-b)=a^2-b^2
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(x+5)(x-5)
\]

\[
=x^2-25
\]

### Worked Example

Expand:

\[
(3x+2)(3x-2)
\]

\[
=(3x)^2-2^2
\]

\[
=9x^2-4
\]

### Common Mistakes
- This formula only works when the two brackets are the same except for the sign.
- There is no middle term because the middle terms cancel out.

---

# 3. Factorisation

## Concept: Factorisation
Factorisation is the reverse of expansion. It means writing an expression as a product of factors.

Example:

\[
3x+6
\]

can be factorised as:

\[
3(x+2)
\]

because:

\[
3(x+2)=3x+6
\]

---

## Factorising Using Common Factor

### Key Method
1. Find the greatest common factor.
2. Take it outside the bracket.
3. Write what remains inside the bracket.

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
6x+12
\]

The common factor is \(6\).

\[
6x+12=6(x+2)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
8a^2-12a
\]

The common factor is \(4a\).

\[
8a^2-12a=4a(2a-3)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
-5x+10
\]

The common factor can be \(-5\).

\[
-5x+10=-5(x-2)
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Always check by expanding your answer.
- Do not remove only the number if the variable is also common.
- For \(8a^2-12a\), the common factor is \(4a\), not only \(4\).

---

## Factorising Quadratic Expressions

A quadratic expression usually has the form:

\[
x^2+bx+c
\]

### Key Method
To factorise:

\[
x^2+bx+c
\]

find two numbers that:
- multiply to give \(c\),
- add to give \(b\).

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
x^2+7x+12
\]

Find two numbers that multiply to \(12\) and add to \(7\).

\[
3 \times 4=12
\]

\[
3+4=7
\]

So:

\[
x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
x^2-5x+6
\]

Find two numbers that multiply to \(6\) and add to \(-5\).

\[
-2 \times -3=6
\]

\[
-2+(-3)=-5
\]

So:

\[
x^2-5x+6=(x-2)(x-3)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
x^2+x-12
\]

Find two numbers that multiply to \(-12\) and add to \(1\).

\[
4 \times -3=-12
\]

\[
4+(-3)=1
\]

So:

\[
x^2+x-12=(x+4)(x-3)
\]

### Common Mistakes
- If \(c\) is positive and \(b\) is negative, both factors are negative.
- If \(c\) is negative, one factor is positive and one factor is negative.
- Always check by expanding.

---

## Factorising Difference of Two Squares

### Formula

\[
a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
x^2-25
\]

\[
x^2-25=x^2-5^2
\]

\[
=(x+5)(x-5)
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
9x^2-16
\]

\[
9x^2-16=(3x)^2-4^2
\]

\[
=(3x+4)(3x-4)
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Difference of two squares needs subtraction.
- \(x^2+25\) cannot be factorised using this formula.
- Both terms must be perfect squares.

---

## Factorising Perfect Square Trinomials

### Formula 1

\[
a^2+2ab+b^2=(a+b)^2
\]

### Formula 2

\[
a^2-2ab+b^2=(a-b)^2
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
x^2+10x+25
\]

\[
x^2+10x+25=x^2+2(x)(5)+5^2
\]

\[
=(x+5)^2
\]

### Worked Example

Factorise:

\[
4x^2-12x+9
\]

\[
4x^2-12x+9=(2x)^2-2(2x)(3)+3^2
\]

\[
=(2x-3)^2
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Check whether the first and last terms are perfect squares.
- Check whether the middle term is \(2ab\).
- Do not assume every three-term expression is a perfect square.

---

# 4. Basic Linear Equations

## Concept: Linear Equation
A linear equation is an equation where the variable has power \(1\).

Examples:

\[
x+3=8
\]

\[
2y-5=11
\]

\[
3a+4=2a+9
\]

The goal is to find the value of the variable.

---

## Solving One-Step Equations

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
x+7=12
\]

Subtract \(7\) from both sides:

\[
x=12-7
\]

\[
x=5
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
3x=18
\]

Divide both sides by \(3\):

\[
x=\frac{18}{3}
\]

\[
x=6
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other side.
- To undo addition, subtract.
- To undo multiplication, divide.

---

## Solving Two-Step Equations

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
2x+5=17
\]

Subtract \(5\) from both sides:

\[
2x=12
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
x=6
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
5y-4=21
\]

Add \(4\) to both sides:

\[
5y=25
\]

Divide by \(5\):

\[
y=5
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Remove addition or subtraction first.
- Then remove multiplication or division.
- Do not divide before moving the constant term unless you know what you are doing.

---

## Solving Equations with Variables on Both Sides

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
3x+4=x+10
\]

Move \(x\) to the left side:

\[
3x-x+4=10
\]

\[
2x+4=10
\]

Subtract \(4\):

\[
2x=6
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
x=3
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
5a-7=2a+8
\]

Move \(2a\) to the left:

\[
5a-2a-7=8
\]

\[
3a-7=8
\]

Add \(7\):

\[
3a=15
\]

Divide by \(3\):

\[
a=5
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Keep variables on one side and constants on the other side.
- Be careful when moving negative terms.
- Check your answer by substituting back into the original equation.

---

## Solving Equations with Brackets

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
2(x+3)=14
\]

Expand:

\[
2x+6=14
\]

Subtract \(6\):

\[
2x=8
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
x=4
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
3(2x-1)=15
\]

Expand:

\[
6x-3=15
\]

Add \(3\):

\[
6x=18
\]

Divide by \(6\):

\[
x=3
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Expand the bracket carefully first.
- If a negative number is outside the bracket, multiply every term by the negative number.
- Do not cancel terms incorrectly.

---

## Solving Equations with Fractions

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
\frac{x}{3}=7
\]

Multiply both sides by \(3\):

\[
x=21
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
\frac{x+2}{5}=4
\]

Multiply both sides by \(5\):

\[
x+2=20
\]

Subtract \(2\):

\[
x=18
\]

### Worked Example

Solve:

\[
\frac{2x-1}{3}=5
\]

Multiply both sides by \(3\):

\[
2x-1=15
\]

Add \(1\):

\[
2x=16
\]

Divide by \(2\):

\[
x=8
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Multiply the whole fraction equation by the denominator.
- If the numerator has more than one term, keep it together.
- \(\frac{x+2}{5}=4\) means the whole \(x+2\) is divided by \(5\).

---

## Forming Linear Equations from Word Problems

### Common Keywords

| Phrase | Mathematical Meaning |
|---|---|
| A number increased by 5 | \(x+5\) |
| A number decreased by 5 | \(x-5\) |
| Twice a number | \(2x\) |
| Three times a number | \(3x\) |
| Half of a number | \(\frac{x}{2}\) |
| The sum of two numbers | \(x+y\) |
| The difference between two numbers | \(x-y\) |

### Worked Example

A number increased by \(8\) is \(20\). Find the number.

Let the number be \(x\).

\[
x+8=20
\]

\[
x=12
\]

### Worked Example

Twice a number decreased by \(5\) is \(17\). Find the number.

Let the number be \(x\).

\[
2x-5=17
\]

\[
2x=22
\]

\[
x=11
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Define the variable clearly.
- Translate the sentence carefully.
- “Twice a number decreased by 5” means \(2x-5\), not \(2(x-5)\), unless the wording says “twice the difference”.

# 5. Basic Laws of Indices

## Concept: Index Notation
Index notation is used for repeated multiplication.

\[
a^n
\]

means \(a\) is multiplied by itself \(n\) times.

Example:

\[
2^4=2\times2\times2\times2=16
\]

where:
- \(2\) is the base,
- \(4\) is the index or power.

---

## Multiplication Law

\[
a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
x^3 \times x^4=x^{3+4}=x^7
\]

### Common Mistake

\[
x^3 \times y^4
\]

cannot be simplified using this law because the bases are different.

---

## Division Law

\[
a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
x^8 \div x^3=x^{8-3}=x^5
\]

### Common Mistake
Only use this when the bases are the same.

---

## Power of a Power

\[
(a^m)^n=a^{mn}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
(x^2)^5=x^{10}
\]

---

## Power of a Product

\[
(ab)^n=a^n b^n
\]

### Worked Example

\[
(3x)^2=3^2x^2=9x^2
\]

### Common Mistake

\[
(3x)^2
\]

is not \(3x^2\). The \(3\) must also be squared.

---

## Zero Index

\[
a^0=1
\]

where:

\[
a\ne0
\]

### Worked Example

\[
7^0=1
\]

\[
x^0=1,\quad x\ne0
\]

### Common Mistake
Any non-zero number to the power \(0\) is \(1\), not \(0\).

---

## Negative Index

\[
a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
2^{-3}=\frac{1}{2^3}=\frac{1}{8}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
x^{-4}=\frac{1}{x^4}
\]

### Common Mistake
A negative index does not mean the value is negative. It means reciprocal.

---

## Fractional Index

\[
a^{\frac{1}{n}}=\sqrt[n]{a}
\]

\[
a^{\frac{m}{n}}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}
\]

### Worked Example

\[
16^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{16}=4
\]

### Worked Example

\[
27^{\frac{2}{3}}=(\sqrt[3]{27})^2
\]

\[
=3^2
\]

\[
=9
\]

### Common Mistakes
- The denominator of the fractional index shows the root.
- The numerator shows the power.
- \(a^{\frac{1}{2}}\) means square root, not divide by \(2\).

---

## Simplifying Index Expressions

### Worked Example

Simplify:

\[
\frac{x^6 \times x^2}{x^3}
\]

First multiply the top:

\[
x^6 \times x^2=x^8
\]

Then divide:

\[
\frac{x^8}{x^3}=x^5
\]

### Worked Example

Simplify:

\[
(2x^3)^2
\]

\[
=2^2(x^3)^2
\]

\[
=4x^6
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Apply index laws step by step.
- Remember to square coefficients.
- Keep bases the same when applying multiplication and division laws.

---

# 6. Basic Polygon Geometry

## Concept: Polygon
A polygon is a closed shape made up of straight sides.

Examples:
- triangle,
- quadrilateral,
- pentagon,
- hexagon,
- heptagon,
- octagon.

### Common Mistakes
- A circle is not a polygon because it has a curved edge.
- A polygon must be closed.
- A polygon must have straight sides.

---

## Common Polygon Names

| Number of Sides | Name |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Triangle |
| 4 | Quadrilateral |
| 5 | Pentagon |
| 6 | Hexagon |
| 7 | Heptagon |
| 8 | Octagon |
| 9 | Nonagon |
| 10 | Decagon |

---

## Triangle Angle Sum

### Key Rule

\[
\text{Sum of angles in a triangle}=180^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

Find \(x\):

\[
x+60^\circ+45^\circ=180^\circ
\]

\[
x+105^\circ=180^\circ
\]

\[
x=75^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- The three interior angles of any triangle always add up to \(180^\circ\).
- This applies to all types of triangles.

---

## Quadrilateral Angle Sum

### Key Rule

\[
\text{Sum of angles in a quadrilateral}=360^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

Find \(x\):

\[
x+80^\circ+95^\circ+110^\circ=360^\circ
\]

\[
x+285^\circ=360^\circ
\]

\[
x=75^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- A quadrilateral has 4 sides.
- The interior angle sum is \(360^\circ\), not \(180^\circ\).

---

## Sum of Interior Angles of a Polygon

### Formula

\[
\text{Sum of interior angles}=(n-2)\times180^\circ
\]

where:
- \(n\) = number of sides.

### Worked Example

Find the sum of interior angles of a pentagon.

A pentagon has \(5\) sides.

\[
(n-2)\times180^\circ=(5-2)\times180^\circ
\]

\[
=3\times180^\circ
\]

\[
=540^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

Find the sum of interior angles of an octagon.

An octagon has \(8\) sides.

\[
(8-2)\times180^\circ=6\times180^\circ
\]

\[
=1080^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Use \(n\) as the number of sides, not the number of angles written in the diagram.
- Do not forget to subtract \(2\) before multiplying by \(180^\circ\).

---

## Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon

A regular polygon has:
- all sides equal,
- all interior angles equal.

### Formula

\[
\text{Each interior angle}
=
\frac{(n-2)\times180^\circ}{n}
\]

### Worked Example

Find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.

A hexagon has \(6\) sides.

\[
\frac{(6-2)\times180^\circ}{6}
\]

\[
=\frac{4\times180^\circ}{6}
\]

\[
=\frac{720^\circ}{6}
\]

\[
=120^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- This formula is for regular polygons only.
- If the polygon is not regular, the angles may not be equal.

---

## Exterior Angle of a Regular Polygon

### Formula

\[
\text{Each exterior angle}=\frac{360^\circ}{n}
\]

where \(n\) is the number of sides.

### Worked Example

Find each exterior angle of a regular octagon.

\[
\frac{360^\circ}{8}=45^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- The sum of exterior angles of any polygon is \(360^\circ\).
- For a regular polygon, each exterior angle is equal.

---

## Relationship Between Interior and Exterior Angles

For a regular polygon:

\[
\text{Interior angle}+\text{Exterior angle}=180^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

If the exterior angle of a regular polygon is \(30^\circ\), find the interior angle.

\[
180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ
\]

So the interior angle is:

\[
150^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

If each exterior angle of a regular polygon is \(24^\circ\), find the number of sides.

\[
n=\frac{360^\circ}{24^\circ}
\]

\[
n=15
\]

So the polygon has \(15\) sides.

### Common Mistakes
- Interior and exterior angles at the same vertex form a straight line.
- Do not confuse total exterior angle sum with each exterior angle.

---

## Angles on a Straight Line

### Key Rule

\[
\text{Angles on a straight line}=180^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

Find \(x\):

\[
x+65^\circ=180^\circ
\]

\[
x=115^\circ
\]

---

## Angles Around a Point

### Key Rule

\[
\text{Angles around a point}=360^\circ
\]

### Worked Example

Find \(x\):

\[
x+90^\circ+120^\circ=360^\circ
\]

\[
x+210^\circ=360^\circ
\]

\[
x=150^\circ
\]

---

## Vertically Opposite Angles

Vertically opposite angles are equal.

### Example

If one angle is \(70^\circ\), the vertically opposite angle is also:

\[
70^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Vertically opposite angles are across from each other.
- Adjacent angles on a straight line are not necessarily equal.

---

## Angles in Parallel Lines

When two parallel lines are cut by a transversal:

### Corresponding Angles

Corresponding angles are equal.

### Alternate Angles

Alternate angles are equal.

### Co-interior Angles

Co-interior angles add up to:

\[
180^\circ
\]

### Common Mistakes
- Use the correct angle relationship.
- If lines are not parallel, these rules may not apply.
- Look for arrow markings that show parallel lines.

# 9. Common Mistakes Summary

## Algebra
- Combining unlike terms.
- Expanding only the first term in a bracket.
- Losing negative signs.
- Forgetting to check factorisation by expanding.

## Equations
- Moving terms without changing signs correctly.
- Dividing only one term instead of the whole side.
- Not expanding brackets before solving.
- Not checking the final answer.

## Indices
- Adding powers when bases are different.
- Thinking negative index means negative value.
- Forgetting to square coefficients.
- Confusing \(a^{1/2}\) with \(\frac{a}{2}\).

## Geometry
- Using \(180^\circ\) for quadrilaterals instead of \(360^\circ\).
- Forgetting that polygon formula uses \(n-2\).
- Using regular polygon formulas for non-regular polygons.
- Confusing interior angle with exterior angle.
- Applying parallel line angle rules when lines are not parallel.

# 10. Mini Formula Summary

## Algebra Expansion

\[
a(b+c)=ab+ac
\]

\[
(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd
\]

\[
(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2
