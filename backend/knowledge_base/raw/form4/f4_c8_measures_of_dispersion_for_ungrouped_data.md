# Topic: Measures of Dispersion for Ungrouped Data
**Form:** Form 4 | **Chapter:** Chapter 8 | **Curriculum:** SPM KSSM

## Overview
Measures of dispersion are used to describe how spread out a set of data is. In this topic, students learn about dispersion, stem-and-leaf plots, dot plots, range, interquartile range, variance, standard deviation, box plots, and the effects of data changes on dispersion.

## Concept: Dispersion
Dispersion describes how scattered or spread out the values in a set of data are.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Measures of dispersion give an idea of how values in a data set are distributed.
- Dispersion is small when the data values are close together.
- Dispersion is large when the data values are far apart.
- A small range means the data is less dispersed.
- A large range means the data is more widely dispersed.

Common measures of dispersion include:
- Range
- Interquartile range
- Variance
- Standard deviation

### Worked Example:
The masses, in kg, of $20$ pupils are:

$$
52,\ 60,\ 62,\ 47,\ 55,\ 48,\ 70,\ 67,\ 71,\ 66
$$

$$
48,\ 50,\ 64,\ 51,\ 66,\ 79,\ 62,\ 65,\ 78,\ 72
$$

Find the difference between the largest and smallest mass.

Largest mass:

$$
79\text{ kg}
$$

Smallest mass:

$$
47\text{ kg}
$$

Difference:

$$
79 - 47 = 32
$$

Therefore, the difference in mass is:

$$
32\text{ kg}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Dispersion is about the spread of data, not only the average.
- A higher dispersion means the data values vary more.
- A lower dispersion means the data values are more consistent.

## Concept: Difference Between Largest and Smallest Values
A simple way to understand dispersion is to compare the largest and smallest values.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Identify the largest value.
- Identify the smallest value.
- Subtract the smallest value from the largest value.

$$
\text{Difference} = \text{Largest value} - \text{Smallest value}
$$

### Worked Example:
A dot plot shows that the longest insect is $6.8$ cm and the shortest insect is $3.1$ cm.

Difference in length:

$$
6.8 - 3.1 = 3.7
$$

Therefore, the difference in length is:

$$
3.7\text{ cm}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Always subtract the smallest value from the largest value.
- For dot plots, read the scale carefully.
- The largest and smallest values may not appear equally often.

## Concept: Stem-and-Leaf Plot
A stem-and-leaf plot is a way to organise and display data so that the distribution can be seen clearly.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- The stem usually represents the tens digit.
- The leaf usually represents the units digit.
- Leaf digits are arranged from smallest to largest.
- A key must be provided to explain the meaning of the stem and leaf.

### Example:
For the data value $27$:

$$
2|7
$$

means:

$$
27
$$

where $2$ is the stem and $7$ is the leaf.

### Worked Example:
The marks obtained by $36$ pupils are:

$$
27,\ 34,\ 37,\ 39,\ 42,\ 43,\ 46,\ 48,\ 52
$$

$$
29,\ 35,\ 37,\ 40,\ 42,\ 44,\ 47,\ 49,\ 52
$$

$$
31,\ 35,\ 38,\ 40,\ 42,\ 44,\ 47,\ 49,\ 53
$$

$$
32,\ 36,\ 38,\ 41,\ 42,\ 45,\ 47,\ 52,\ 54
$$

The stem-and-leaf plot is:

| Stem | Leaf |
|---|---|
| $2$ | $7,\ 9$ |
| $3$ | $1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 7,\ 8,\ 8,\ 9$ |
| $4$ | $0,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 7,\ 7,\ 8,\ 9,\ 9$ |
| $5$ | $2,\ 2,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4$ |

Key:

$$
2|7 \text{ means } 27
$$

From the stem-and-leaf plot, most marks are in the range $40$ to $49$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not forget the key.
- Arrange the leaves in ascending order.
- Use the same stem scale when comparing two data sets.
- A stem-and-leaf plot still keeps the original data values.

## Concept: Dot Plot
A dot plot is a statistical chart that uses dots to represent data values on a uniform scale.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Each dot represents one data value.
- The values are plotted on a number line.
- Repeated values are shown by stacking dots above the same value.
- Dot plots are suitable for small data sets.
- Dot plots make it easy to identify:
  - Highest value
  - Lowest value
  - Most frequent value
  - Spread of data

### Worked Example:
The scores obtained by $20$ pupils in a Biology quiz are:

$$
0,\ 6,\ 4,\ 7,\ 6
$$

$$
2,\ 0,\ 1,\ 7,\ 7
$$

$$
4,\ 5,\ 2,\ 3,\ 8
$$

$$
7,\ 8,\ 3,\ 5,\ 3
$$

From the dot plot:
- The lowest score is $0$.
- The highest score is $8$.
- The score that appears most often is $7$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Each dot represents one data value.
- Use a uniform scale.
- Do not skip values on the number line without marking the scale clearly.
- Dot plots are best for small data sets.

## Concept: Comparing Data Using Stem-and-Leaf Plots
Two or more sets of data can be compared using stem-and-leaf plots.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
When comparing stem-and-leaf plots, consider:
- The range of values.
- Where most values are concentrated.
- Whether the data is more spread out or less spread out.
- Whether one data set generally has higher values than another.
- The most frequent range of values.

### Example Interpretation:
If one test has more marks concentrated in higher stems, the pupils performed better in that test.

If another test has marks spread across a wider range, the dispersion is larger.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not compare only the highest value.
- Look at the full distribution.
- Check whether most values are on the lower side or higher side.
- A wider spread means greater dispersion.

## Concept: Comparing Data Using Dot Plots
Dot plots can be used to compare the dispersion and performance of two data sets.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
When comparing dot plots, consider:
- Shape of the distribution.
- Spread of the dots.
- Frequency of repeated values.
- Values concentrated on the left or right.
- Difference between the highest and lowest values.

### Worked Example:
A Mathematics quiz and a Science quiz are taken by $20$ pupils.

From the dot plots:
- The Science quiz has a larger dispersion because it has a larger difference in values.
- The Science quiz has a higher difference in value, which is $7$.
- The Mathematics quiz shows better achievement because most scores are concentrated on the right side of the dot plot.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- If most values are on the right side, the achievement is generally better.
- If values are spread widely, the dispersion is larger.
- The highest score alone does not determine better achievement.
- Compare both spread and concentration of values.

## Concept: Range
The range is the difference between the largest value and the smallest value in a data set.

**Key Rules / Methods:**

$$
\text{Range} = \text{Largest value} - \text{Smallest value}
$$

- Range is the easiest measure of dispersion to calculate.
- A larger range means greater dispersion.
- A smaller range means smaller dispersion.

### Worked Example:
Given the data:

$$
34,\ 23,\ 14,\ 26,\ 40,\ 25,\ 20
$$

Largest value:

$$
40
$$

Smallest value:

$$
14
$$

Range:

$$
40 - 14 = 26
$$

Therefore, the range is:

$$
26
$$

### Worked Example:
The table shows scores obtained by pupils.

| Score | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Number of pupils | $3$ | $5$ | $6$ | $8$ | $12$ | $7$ | $3$ |

Largest value:

$$
8
$$

Smallest value:

$$
2
$$

Range:

$$
8 - 2 = 6
$$

Therefore, the range is:

$$
6
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- In a frequency table, use the largest and smallest data values, not the largest and smallest frequencies.
- Range is affected strongly by outliers.
- Range does not show how values are distributed between the smallest and largest values.

## Concept: Quartiles
Quartiles divide a data set into four parts after the data values are arranged in order.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- The first quartile is written as $Q_1$.
- The second quartile is the median and is written as $Q_2$.
- The third quartile is written as $Q_3$.
- $Q_1$ is also called the lower quartile.
- $Q_3$ is also called the upper quartile.
- The data must be arranged in ascending order first.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Always arrange the data before finding quartiles.
- The median is $Q_2$.
- Do not find quartiles from unsorted data.

## Concept: Interquartile Range
The interquartile range measures the spread of the middle half of the data.

**Key Rules / Methods:**

$$
\text{Interquartile range} = Q_3 - Q_1
$$

Steps to find $Q_1$ and $Q_3$:
1. Arrange the data in ascending order.
2. Find the median.
3. Divide the data into two parts:
   - Data before the median.
   - Data after the median.
4. $Q_1$ is the median of the lower part.
5. $Q_3$ is the median of the upper part.
6. If the number of data values is even, take the average of the two middle values.

### Worked Example:
Find the interquartile range of:

$$
4,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 13,\ 15,\ 16,\ 20,\ 22,\ 23
$$

The data is already arranged in ascending order.

Median:

$$
13
$$

Lower part:

$$
4,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10
$$

So:

$$
Q_1 = 6
$$

Upper part:

$$
15,\ 16,\ 20,\ 22,\ 23
$$

So:

$$
Q_3 = 20
$$

Interquartile range:

$$
Q_3 - Q_1 = 20 - 6
$$

$$
= 14
$$

### Worked Example:
Find the interquartile range of:

$$
11,\ 14,\ 14,\ 17,\ 19,\ 20,\ 20,\ 21,\ 22
$$

Median:

$$
19
$$

Lower part:

$$
11,\ 14,\ 14,\ 17
$$

$$
Q_1 = \frac{14 + 14}{2}
$$

$$
= 14
$$

Upper part:

$$
20,\ 20,\ 21,\ 22
$$

$$
Q_3 = \frac{20 + 21}{2}
$$

$$
= 20.5
$$

Interquartile range:

$$
20.5 - 14 = 6.5
$$

### Worked Example:
Find the interquartile range of:

$$
2.3,\ 2.5,\ 2.5,\ 2.6,\ 2.8,\ 2.9,\ 3.0,\ 3.2
$$

Lower part:

$$
2.3,\ 2.5,\ 2.5,\ 2.6
$$

$$
Q_1 = \frac{2.5 + 2.5}{2}
$$

$$
= 2.5
$$

Upper part:

$$
2.8,\ 2.9,\ 3.0,\ 3.2
$$

$$
Q_3 = \frac{2.9 + 3.0}{2}
$$

$$
= 2.95
$$

Interquartile range:

$$
2.95 - 2.5 = 0.45
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not include the median when splitting an odd number of data values into lower and upper parts.
- Interquartile range is less affected by outliers than range.
- Always calculate $Q_3 - Q_1$, not $Q_1 - Q_3$.

## Concept: Interquartile Range from a Frequency Table
For a frequency table, cumulative frequency can be used to find $Q_1$ and $Q_3$.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Find the total frequency, $N$.
- Find the cumulative frequency.
- Determine the position of $Q_1$:

$$
Q_1 = \frac{1}{4}N\text{th value}
$$

- Determine the position of $Q_3$:

$$
Q_3 = \frac{3}{4}N\text{th value}
$$

- Use cumulative frequency to identify the data values at those positions.

### Worked Example:
The table shows the scores of pupils.

| Score | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Number of pupils | $3$ | $5$ | $6$ | $8$ | $12$ | $7$ | $3$ |
| Cumulative frequency | $3$ | $8$ | $14$ | $22$ | $34$ | $41$ | $44$ |

Total frequency:

$$
N = 44
$$

Position of $Q_1$:

$$
\frac{1}{4} \times 44 = 11
$$

So $Q_1$ is the $11$th value.

From the cumulative frequency, the $11$th value is:

$$
4
$$

Position of $Q_3$:

$$
\frac{3}{4} \times 44 = 33
$$

So $Q_3$ is the $33$rd value.

From the cumulative frequency, the $33$rd value is:

$$
6
$$

Interquartile range:

$$
Q_3 - Q_1 = 6 - 4
$$

$$
= 2
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use cumulative frequency to locate positions.
- Do not use the frequency value as the data value.
- Check the total frequency before finding quartile positions.

## Concept: Variance
Variance measures how far data values are spread from the mean.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Variance is the average of the square of the difference between each data value and the mean.
- The symbol for variance is:

$$
\sigma^2
$$

For ungrouped data:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N}
$$

Alternative formula:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2
$$

where:
- $x$ is a data value.
- $\bar{x}$ is the mean.
- $N$ is the number of data values.
- $\sum$ means sum of.

### Worked Example:
Find the variance of:

$$
2,\ 4,\ 5,\ 5,\ 6
$$

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{2 + 4 + 5 + 5 + 6}{5}
$$

$$
= 4.4
$$

Variance:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{(2 - 4.4)^2 + (4 - 4.4)^2 + (5 - 4.4)^2 + (5 - 4.4)^2 + (6 - 4.4)^2}{5}
$$

$$
= 1.84
$$

Alternative method:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{2^2 + 4^2 + 5^2 + 5^2 + 6^2}{5} - 4.4^2
$$

$$
= 1.84
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Square the differences before adding them.
- Divide by $N$, not by $N - 1$, for this syllabus formula.
- Variance is in squared units.

## Concept: Standard Deviation
Standard deviation measures the spread of data values from the mean.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Standard deviation is the square root of variance.
- The symbol for standard deviation is:

$$
\sigma
$$

For ungrouped data:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N}}
$$

Alternative formula:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2}
$$

### Worked Example:
Find the standard deviation of:

$$
5,\ 7,\ 8,\ 8,\ 10,\ 13,\ 15,\ 16,\ 16,\ 20
$$

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{5 + 7 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 13 + 15 + 16 + 16 + 20}{10}
$$

$$
= 11.8
$$

Variance:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{5^2 + 7^2 + 8^2 + 8^2 + 10^2 + 13^2 + 15^2 + 16^2 + 16^2 + 20^2}{10} - 11.8^2
$$

$$
= 21.56
$$

Standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{21.56}
$$

$$
= 4.643
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Standard deviation is the square root of variance.
- Standard deviation uses the same unit as the original data.
- A smaller standard deviation means the data is more consistent.
- A larger standard deviation means the data is more spread out.

## Concept: Variance and Standard Deviation from a Frequency Table
When data is given in a frequency table, use frequency in the calculation.

**Key Rules / Methods:**

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}
$$

Variance:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum f(x - \bar{x})^2}{\sum f}
$$

Alternative formula:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \bar{x}^2
$$

Standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}
$$

### Worked Example:
The table shows the number of books read by pupils in a month.

| Number of books, $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Number of pupils, $f$ | $3$ | $5$ | $8$ | $2$ | $2$ |

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{3(0) + 5(1) + 8(2) + 2(3) + 2(4)}{3 + 5 + 8 + 2 + 2}
$$

$$
= \frac{35}{20}
$$

$$
= 1.75
$$

Variance:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{3(0 - 1.75)^2 + 5(1 - 1.75)^2 + 8(2 - 1.75)^2 + 2(3 - 1.75)^2 + 2(4 - 1.75)^2}{20}
$$

$$
= 1.2875
$$

Alternative method:

| $x$ | $f$ | $fx$ | $x^2$ | $fx^2$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $0$ | $3$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |
| $1$ | $5$ | $5$ | $1$ | $5$ |
| $2$ | $8$ | $16$ | $4$ | $32$ |
| $3$ | $2$ | $6$ | $9$ | $18$ |
| $4$ | $2$ | $8$ | $16$ | $32$ |

$$
\sum f = 20
$$

$$
\sum fx = 35
$$

$$
\sum fx^2 = 87
$$

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{35}{20} = 1.75
$$

Variance:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{87}{20} - 1.75^2
$$

$$
= 1.2875
$$

Standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{1.2875}
$$

$$
= 1.1347
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use $\sum f$ as the total number of data values.
- Do not forget to multiply by frequency.
- In the alternative formula, use $\sum fx^2$, not $(\sum fx)^2$.
- Standard deviation is obtained after taking the square root of variance.

## Concept: Choosing a Suitable Measure of Dispersion
Different measures of dispersion are useful in different situations.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Range is easy to calculate but is strongly affected by outliers.
- Interquartile range is useful when there are outliers because it focuses on the middle half of the data.
- Standard deviation is useful for comparing consistency between two data sets.
- A low standard deviation means values are closer to the mean.
- A high standard deviation means values are further from the mean.

### Worked Example:
Given the data:

$$
10,\ 11,\ 11,\ 14,\ 18,\ 20,\ 21,\ 21,\ 25,\ 40
$$

Range:

$$
40 - 10 = 30
$$

Interquartile range:

$$
21 - 11 = 10
$$

The value $40$ is an outlier.

Therefore, the interquartile range is more appropriate because it is less affected by the outlier.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not always choose range just because it is easy.
- If there is an outlier, interquartile range is usually more suitable.
- If comparing consistency, standard deviation is usually more suitable.

## Concept: Advantages and Disadvantages of Measures of Dispersion
Each measure of dispersion has strengths and weaknesses.

| Measure | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Easy to calculate | Strongly affected by outliers |
| Interquartile range | Less affected by outliers | Does not use all data values |
| Variance | Uses all data values | Unit is squared, so interpretation is less direct |
| Standard deviation | Uses all data values and compares consistency well | Calculation is longer |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use range for a quick overall spread.
- Use interquartile range when outliers exist.
- Use standard deviation when comparing consistency of two or more data sets.
- Explain why the chosen measure is suitable.

## Concept: Comparing Consistency Using Standard Deviation
Standard deviation can be used to compare the consistency of two data sets.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If two data sets have the same mean, the one with smaller standard deviation is more consistent.
- Smaller standard deviation means the values are closer to the mean.
- Larger standard deviation means the values vary more.

### Worked Example:
The table shows the marks of two pupils in $5$ Physics tests.

| Pupil | Test 1 | Test 2 | Test 3 | Test 4 | Test 5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Aiman | $40$ | $70$ | $90$ | $85$ | $64$ |
| John | $80$ | $65$ | $73$ | $58$ | $73$ |

Aiman’s mean:

$$
\frac{40 + 70 + 90 + 85 + 64}{5} = 69.8
$$

Aiman’s standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = 17.67
$$

John’s mean:

$$
\frac{80 + 65 + 73 + 58 + 73}{5} = 69.8
$$

John’s standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = 7.574
$$

Both pupils have the same mean, but John has a smaller standard deviation.

Therefore, John is more consistent and should be selected.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Same mean does not mean same consistency.
- Compare standard deviation to judge consistency.
- The smaller standard deviation shows more stable performance.

## Concept: Box Plot
A box plot shows the distribution of a data set using five important values.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
A box plot is based on:
- Minimum value
- First quartile, $Q_1$
- Median
- Third quartile, $Q_3$
- Maximum value

A box plot can show:
- The spread of the data.
- The interquartile range.
- Whether the data is symmetric around the median.
- Possible skewness of the data.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A box plot is drawn on a number line.
- The box goes from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$.
- The line inside the box is the median.
- The whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum values.

## Concept: Constructing a Box Plot
To construct a box plot, first find the five-number summary.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
Steps:
1. Arrange the data in ascending order.
2. Find the minimum value.
3. Find $Q_1$.
4. Find the median.
5. Find $Q_3$.
6. Find the maximum value.
7. Draw a number line.
8. Draw the box from $Q_1$ to $Q_3$.
9. Mark the median inside the box.
10. Draw whiskers to the minimum and maximum values.

### Worked Example:
The marks obtained by $15$ pupils are:

$$
35,\ 40,\ 42,\ 45,\ 52,\ 52,\ 53,\ 57,\ 62,\ 62,\ 66,\ 68,\ 73,\ 73,\ 75
$$

Five-number summary:

| Value | Result |
|---|---:|
| Minimum value | $35$ |
| First quartile, $Q_1$ | $45$ |
| Median | $57$ |
| Third quartile, $Q_3$ | $68$ |
| Maximum value | $75$ |

Interquartile range:

$$
68 - 45 = 23
$$

The box plot has:
- Left whisker at $35$.
- Box from $45$ to $68$.
- Median line at $57$.
- Right whisker at $75$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not use the mean in a box plot unless asked separately.
- The box represents the middle $50\%$ of the data.
- The whiskers represent the spread from minimum to $Q_1$ and from $Q_3$ to maximum.

## Concept: Interpreting a Box Plot
A box plot can be interpreted by reading its five-number summary.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
From a box plot, identify:
- Minimum value
- Maximum value
- $Q_1$
- Median
- $Q_3$
- Range
- Interquartile range

Formulas:

$$
\text{Range} = \text{Maximum value} - \text{Minimum value}
$$

$$
\text{Interquartile range} = Q_3 - Q_1
$$

### Worked Example:
A box plot has:
- Minimum value $18$
- $Q_1 = 35$
- Median $52$
- $Q_3 = 64$
- Maximum value $80$

Interquartile range:

$$
64 - 35 = 29
$$

Range:

$$
80 - 18 = 62
$$

### Worked Example:
A box plot has:
- Minimum value $0$
- $Q_1 = 5$
- Median $11$
- $Q_3 = 21$
- Maximum value $35$

Interquartile range:

$$
21 - 5 = 16
$$

Range:

$$
35 - 0 = 35
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Read values from the scale carefully.
- Range uses maximum and minimum.
- Interquartile range uses $Q_3$ and $Q_1$.
- The median is not always in the middle of the box.

## Concept: Effects of Adding or Subtracting a Constant
When each value in a data set is added to or subtracted by the same constant, the measures of dispersion do not change.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
If each data value is added by a constant $k$:
- Range remains the same.
- Interquartile range remains the same.
- Variance remains the same.
- Standard deviation remains the same.
- The whole graph shifts to the right by $k$.

If each data value is subtracted by a constant $k$:
- Range remains the same.
- Interquartile range remains the same.
- Variance remains the same.
- Standard deviation remains the same.
- The whole graph shifts to the left by $k$.

### Example:
Original scores have range:

$$
15 - 8 = 7
$$

If each score is added by $2$, the new range is:

$$
17 - 10 = 7
$$

The range remains unchanged.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Adding or subtracting a constant changes the location of the data, not the spread.
- The mean changes, but the dispersion does not.
- A dot plot shifts left or right, but the shape remains the same.

## Concept: Effects of Multiplying or Dividing by a Constant
When each value in a data set is multiplied or divided by a constant, the measures of dispersion change.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
If each data value is multiplied by $k$:
- New range:

$$
k \times \text{original range}
$$

- New interquartile range:

$$
k \times \text{original interquartile range}
$$

- New standard deviation:

$$
k \times \text{original standard deviation}
$$

- New variance:

$$
k^2 \times \text{original variance}
$$

If each data value is divided by $k$:
- New range:

$$
\frac{\text{original range}}{k}
$$

- New interquartile range:

$$
\frac{\text{original interquartile range}}{k}
$$

- New standard deviation:

$$
\frac{\text{original standard deviation}}{k}
$$

- New variance:

$$
\frac{\text{original variance}}{k^2}
$$

### Example:
Original range:

$$
5 - 1 = 4
$$

If each value is multiplied by $2$, new range:

$$
10 - 2 = 8
$$

The new range is:

$$
2 \times 4 = 8
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Variance is multiplied by $k^2$, not $k$.
- Standard deviation is multiplied by $k$.
- Adding a constant does not change dispersion, but multiplying by a constant does.

## Concept: Effects of Outliers
An outlier is an extreme value that is very different from the other values in a data set.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Adding an outlier usually increases the dispersion.
- Removing an outlier usually decreases the dispersion.
- Range is strongly affected by outliers.
- Interquartile range is less affected by outliers.
- Variance and standard deviation can increase significantly when an outlier is added.

### Worked Example:
Given the data:

$$
4,\ 6,\ 7,\ 7,\ 9,\ 11,\ 12,\ 12
$$

Mean:

$$
\bar{x} = 8.5
$$

Standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = 2.784
$$

If $4$ is removed, the new standard deviation is:

$$
2.356
$$

The standard deviation becomes smaller because $4$ is far from the mean.

If $7$ is added, the new standard deviation is:

$$
2.668
$$

The standard deviation becomes smaller because $7$ is close to the mean.

If $18$ is added, the new standard deviation is:

$$
3.974
$$

The standard deviation becomes larger because $18$ is far from the mean.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Outliers affect range and standard deviation strongly.
- Interquartile range is usually better when outliers exist.
- A value close to the mean may reduce the standard deviation when added.
- A value far from the mean may increase the standard deviation when added.

## Concept: Effects of Data Changes on Graphical Representation
Changes in data can be shown using dot plots, stem-and-leaf plots, or box plots.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Adding a constant shifts the graph without changing its spread.
- Subtracting a constant shifts the graph without changing its spread.
- Multiplying by a constant stretches the graph.
- Dividing by a constant compresses the graph.
- Adding an outlier makes the graph more spread out.
- Removing an outlier may make the graph less spread out.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A shift does not change dispersion.
- A stretch changes dispersion.
- Outliers can make the graph look much wider.
- Use the graphical change to explain changes in range, interquartile range, or standard deviation.

## Concept: Comparing Two or More Sets of Ungrouped Data
Two or more sets of data can be compared using suitable measures of dispersion.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Use range for a quick comparison of spread.
- Use interquartile range when outliers exist.
- Use standard deviation to compare consistency.
- Use mean together with standard deviation when comparing performance and consistency.
- The conclusion must match the context of the question.

### Worked Example:
The table shows the time taken by employees in two factories to complete a job.

| Time (hours) | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Factory A | $3$ | $2$ | $9$ | $2$ | $4$ |
| Factory B | $1$ | $5$ | $7$ | $5$ | $2$ |

Factory A mean:

$$
\bar{x} = 7.1
$$

Factory A standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = 1.261
$$

Factory B mean:

$$
\bar{x} = 7.1
$$

Factory B standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = 1.044
$$

Both factories have the same mean, but Factory B has a smaller standard deviation.

Therefore, Factory B’s employees are more consistent and more efficient.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not compare only the mean.
- If two means are equal, compare standard deviation.
- Smaller standard deviation means more consistent values.
- Always write the conclusion in context.

## Concept: Solving Problems Involving Combined Groups
When two groups of data are combined, use the total sum and total sum of squares.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
For each group:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{N}
$$

so:

$$
\sum x = N\bar{x}
$$

Variance formula:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2
$$

so:

$$
\sum x^2 = N(\sigma^2 + \bar{x}^2)
$$

For combined groups:
- Add the values of $\sum x$.
- Add the values of $\sum x^2$.
- Add the total number of data values.
- Find the new mean.
- Find the new variance or standard deviation.

### Worked Example:
The table shows information about the masses of two groups of pupils.

| Group | Number of pupils | Mean | Variance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | $18$ | $52$ | $2.5$ |
| B | $12$ | $56$ | $1.8$ |

For Group A:

$$
52 = \frac{\sum x}{18}
$$

$$
\sum x = 936
$$

Using:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2
$$

$$
2.5 = \frac{\sum x^2}{18} - 52^2
$$

$$
\sum x^2 = 48717
$$

For Group B:

$$
56 = \frac{\sum x}{12}
$$

$$
\sum x = 672
$$

$$
1.8 = \frac{\sum x^2}{12} - 56^2
$$

$$
\sum x^2 = 37653.6
$$

Combined totals:

$$
\sum x = 936 + 672 = 1608
$$

$$
\sum x^2 = 48717 + 37653.6 = 86370.6
$$

Total number of pupils:

$$
18 + 12 = 30
$$

Combined mean:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{1608}{30}
$$

$$
= 53.6
$$

Combined standard deviation:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{86370.6}{30} - 53.6^2}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{6.06}
$$

$$
= 2.462
$$

Therefore, the standard deviation of the combined groups is:

$$
2.462
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not average the two means directly unless the groups have equal size.
- Do not average the two standard deviations.
- Use total $\sum x$ and total $\sum x^2$ for combined data.
- Check whether the question gives variance or standard deviation.

## Concept: Summary of Key Formulas
The main formulas in this topic are:

### Range

$$
\text{Range} = \text{Largest value} - \text{Smallest value}
$$

### Interquartile Range

$$
\text{Interquartile range} = Q_3 - Q_1
$$

### Mean

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{N}
$$

For frequency table:

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}
$$

### Variance

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N}
$$

Alternative formula:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2
$$

For frequency table:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum f(x - \bar{x})^2}{\sum f}
$$

Alternative frequency formula:

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \bar{x}^2
$$

### Standard Deviation

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{N}}
$$

Alternative formula:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{N} - \bar{x}^2}
$$

For frequency table:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f(x - \bar{x})^2}{\sum f}}
$$

Alternative frequency formula:

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum fx^2}{\sum f} - \bar{x}^2}
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Range and interquartile range measure spread using positions.
- Variance and standard deviation measure spread from the mean.
- Standard deviation is easier to interpret than variance because it uses the original unit.
- Choose the measure of dispersion based on the question context.
