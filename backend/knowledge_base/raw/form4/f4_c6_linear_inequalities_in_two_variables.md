# Topic: Linear Inequalities in Two Variables
**Form:** Form 4 | **Chapter:** Chapter 6 | **Curriculum:** SPM KSSM

## Overview
Linear inequalities in two variables are used to describe relationships between two quantities that are not equal. In this topic, students learn how to form linear inequalities from situations, identify points that satisfy inequalities, shade regions on Cartesian planes, form systems of linear inequalities, and solve real-life problems involving common regions.

## Concept: Linear Inequality in Two Variables
A linear inequality in two variables is an inequality that involves two variables, usually $x$ and $y$, where the highest power of each variable is $1$.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Inequalities are used to compare quantities that are not equal.
- Common inequality symbols are:
  - $>$ means greater than.
  - $<$ means less than.
  - $\geq$ means greater than or equal to.
  - $\leq$ means less than or equal to.
- A linear equation in two variables has the form:

$$
ax + by = c
$$

- A linear inequality in two variables may have the form:

$$
ax + by < c
$$

$$
ax + by > c
$$

$$
ax + by \leq c
$$

$$
ax + by \geq c
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A linear inequality must have variables with highest power $1$.
- The variables must represent quantities with suitable meaning.
- Use $\leq$ or $\geq$ when the situation includes equality, such as “at most”, “not more than”, “at least”, or “not less than”.

## Concept: Representing Situations as Linear Inequalities
A real-life situation can be represented as a linear inequality by assigning variables to the quantities involved.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Define the variables clearly.
- Identify the cost, quantity, limit, or condition.
- Form an inequality based on the situation.
- Use:
  - $\leq$ for “maximum”, “not more than”, “does not exceed”.
  - $\geq$ for “minimum”, “not less than”, “at least”.
  - $>$ for “more than” or “exceeds”.
  - $<$ for “less than”.

### Worked Example:
Taufik wants to buy revision books and exercise books at a book exhibition.

The price of one revision book is RM$14$.

The price of one exercise book is RM$9$.

The maximum amount of money he can spend is RM$100$.

Let:

$$
x = \text{number of revision books}
$$

$$
y = \text{number of exercise books}
$$

The total cost is:

$$
14x + 9y
$$

Since the maximum amount is RM$100$:

$$
14x + 9y \leq 100
$$

The inequality can also be written as:

$$
14x \leq 100 - 9y
$$

or

$$
9y \leq 100 - 14x
$$

### Worked Example:
Puan Hidayah wants to buy small and large mathematics posters.

The price of a small poster is RM$12.50$.

The price of a large poster is RM$18.50$.

The allocation is RM$150$.

Let:

$$
k = \text{number of small posters}
$$

$$
b = \text{number of large posters}
$$

Form the inequality:

$$
12.50k + 18.50b \leq 150
$$

Write the decimals as fractions:

$$
\frac{25}{2}k + \frac{37}{2}b \leq 150
$$

Multiply both sides by $2$:

$$
25k + 37b \leq 300
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Always define the variables first.
- Check whether the situation involves a maximum or minimum.
- If the value is a budget or allocation, usually use $\leq$.
- If the value must exceed a target, use $>$.
- When decimals are inconvenient, multiply to remove decimals or fractions.

## Concept: Interpreting Inequality Phrases
Certain phrases in word problems can be translated directly into inequalities.

**Key Rules / Methods:**

| Situation | Linear Inequality |
|---|---|
| $y$ is greater than $x$ | $y > x$ |
| $y$ is less than $x$ | $y < x$ |
| $y$ is not less than $x$ | $y \geq x$ |
| $y$ is not more than $x$ | $y \leq x$ |
| $y$ is at least $k$ times $x$ | $y \geq kx$ |
| $y$ is at most $k$ times $x$ | $y \leq kx$ |
| Maximum of $y$ is $k$ | $y \leq k$ |
| Minimum of $y$ is $k$ | $y \geq k$ |
| Sum of $x$ and $y$ is greater than $k$ | $x + y > k$ |
| Difference between $y$ and $x$ is less than $k$ | $y - x < k$ |
| $y$ is more than $x$ by at least $k$ | $y - x \geq k$ |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- “At least” means $\geq$.
- “At most” means $\leq$.
- “Not less than” means $\geq$.
- “Not more than” means $\leq$.
- “Exceeds” means $>$.
- “Does not exceed” means $\leq$.

## Concept: Points and Linear Inequalities on a Cartesian Plane
A linear inequality can be represented on a Cartesian plane by using a straight line and shading a region.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
For the straight line:

$$
y = mx + c
$$

- Points on the straight line satisfy:

$$
y = mx + c
$$

- Points above the straight line satisfy:

$$
y > mx + c
$$

- Points below the straight line satisfy:

$$
y < mx + c
$$

For a horizontal line:

$$
y = h
$$

- Points on the line satisfy $y = h$.
- Points above the line satisfy $y > h$.
- Points below the line satisfy $y < h$.

For a vertical line:

$$
x = k
$$

- Points on the line satisfy $x = k$.
- Points to the right of the line satisfy $x > k$.
- Points to the left of the line satisfy $x < k$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Above and below are used for inequalities involving $y$.
- Left and right are used for inequalities involving $x$.
- A horizontal line has equation $y = h$.
- A vertical line has equation $x = k$.
- The gradient of $y = h$ is $0$.
- The gradient of $x = k$ is undefined.

## Concept: Verifying Whether a Point Satisfies a Linear Inequality
A point can be tested by substituting its coordinates into the inequality.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- For a point $(x, y)$, substitute the $x$-coordinate and $y$-coordinate into the equation or inequality.
- Compare the value of $y$ with the value of the expression on the right side.
- If the values are equal, the point lies on the line.
- If $y$ is greater, the point is above the line.
- If $y$ is smaller, the point is below the line.

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
y = -3x + 6
$$

Determine whether the point $(2, 5)$ satisfies $y = -3x + 6$, $y > -3x + 6$, or $y < -3x + 6$.

Substitute $x = 2$:

$$
-3(2) + 6 = -6 + 6
$$

$$
= 0
$$

Compare with $y = 5$:

$$
5 > 0
$$

Therefore, the point $(2, 5)$ satisfies:

$$
y > -3x + 6
$$

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
y = -3x + 6
$$

Determine whether the point $(-1, 9)$ satisfies $y = -3x + 6$, $y > -3x + 6$, or $y < -3x + 6$.

Substitute $x = -1$:

$$
-3(-1) + 6 = 3 + 6
$$

$$
= 9
$$

Compare with $y = 9$:

$$
9 = 9
$$

Therefore, the point $(-1, 9)$ satisfies:

$$
y = -3x + 6
$$

### Worked Example:
Given:

$$
y = -3x + 6
$$

Determine whether the point $(1, 2)$ satisfies $y = -3x + 6$, $y > -3x + 6$, or $y < -3x + 6$.

Substitute $x = 1$:

$$
-3(1) + 6 = 3
$$

Compare with $y = 2$:

$$
2 < 3
$$

Therefore, the point $(1, 2)$ satisfies:

$$
y < -3x + 6
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Always substitute the $x$-coordinate into the expression first.
- Compare the given $y$-coordinate with the calculated value.
- Do not compare the $x$-coordinate with the expression.
- A point on the line satisfies the equation, not a strict inequality.

## Concept: Dashed Line and Solid Line
The type of boundary line depends on the inequality sign.

**Key Rules / Methods:**

| Inequality Sign | Type of Line | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $>$ | Dashed line | Points on the line are not included |
| $<$ | Dashed line | Points on the line are not included |
| $\geq$ | Solid line | Points on the line are included |
| $\leq$ | Solid line | Points on the line are included |

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Use a dashed line for $>$ and $<$.
- Use a solid line for $\geq$ and $\leq$.
- The line itself is included only when the inequality has “or equal to”.

## Concept: Shading the Region of a Linear Inequality
To represent a linear inequality on a Cartesian plane, draw the boundary line and shade the correct region.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Convert the inequality into an equation to draw the boundary line.
- Decide whether the line is dashed or solid.
- Decide whether to shade above, below, left, or right.
- Shade the region that satisfies the inequality.

### General Rules:
For:

$$
y > mx + c
$$

shade the region above the line.

For:

$$
y < mx + c
$$

shade the region below the line.

For:

$$
y \geq mx + c
$$

shade the region above the line, including the line.

For:

$$
y \leq mx + c
$$

shade the region below the line, including the line.

For:

$$
x > k
$$

shade the region to the right of the line.

For:

$$
x < k
$$

shade the region to the left of the line.

For:

$$
x \geq k
$$

shade the region to the right of the line, including the line.

For:

$$
x \leq k
$$

shade the region to the left of the line, including the line.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region:

$$
y > \frac{2}{3}x + 2
$$

Step $1$: Draw the boundary line:

$$
y = \frac{2}{3}x + 2
$$

Step $2$: Since the inequality sign is $>$, draw a dashed line.

Step $3$: Since it is $y > \frac{2}{3}x + 2$, shade the region above the line.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region:

$$
y \leq x - 1
$$

Step $1$: Draw the boundary line:

$$
y = x - 1
$$

Step $2$: Since the inequality sign is $\leq$, draw a solid line.

Step $3$: Since it is $y \leq x - 1$, shade the region below the line.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region:

$$
x > -3
$$

Step $1$: Draw the boundary line:

$$
x = -3
$$

Step $2$: Since the inequality sign is $>$, draw a dashed vertical line.

Step $3$: Shade the region to the right of the line.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Convert the inequality to an equation first before drawing the boundary line.
- For $x > k$ or $x < k$, the boundary line is vertical.
- For $y > h$ or $y < h$, the boundary line is horizontal.
- If the inequality is not in the form $y > mx + c$ or $y < mx + c$, rearrange it first.

## Concept: Rearranging Inequalities Before Shading
Some inequalities must be rearranged before the correct region can be shaded.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Try to make $y$ the subject when shading regions above or below a line.
- If multiplying or dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.
- Convert the inequality into the form:

$$
y > mx + c
$$

or

$$
y < mx + c
$$

when possible.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region:

$$
x - y > 4
$$

Rearrange:

$$
x - y > 4
$$

Subtract $x$ from both sides:

$$
-y > 4 - x
$$

Multiply by $-1$ and reverse the inequality sign:

$$
y < x - 4
$$

Therefore, draw the line:

$$
y = x - 4
$$

Since the inequality is $y < x - 4$, shade the region below the dashed line.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- When multiplying by $-1$, reverse the inequality sign.
- Do not shade before rearranging if the inequality is difficult to interpret.
- For example, $x - y > 4$ becomes $y < x - 4$, not $y > x - 4$.

## Concept: System of Linear Inequalities in Two Variables
A system of linear inequalities is a combination of two or more linear inequalities.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- A system of linear inequalities contains more than one inequality.
- The solution is the common region that satisfies all the inequalities.
- A point is a solution only if it satisfies every inequality in the system.

### Example:
The system:

$$
x + y \leq 8
$$

$$
y \geq 3
$$

has a solution region that satisfies both inequalities at the same time.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A point must satisfy all inequalities, not just one.
- The solution of a system is the overlapping or common region.
- If the variables represent quantities, include:

$$
x \geq 0,\quad y \geq 0
$$

## Concept: Representing Situations as Systems of Linear Inequalities
Real-life problems may require more than one inequality.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Define the variables.
- Translate each condition into one inequality.
- Include $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ when $x$ and $y$ represent quantities.
- Write all inequalities clearly.

### Worked Example:
SMK Seri Permata is hosting a Leadership Camp.

Let:

$$
x = \text{number of Form 5 pupils}
$$

$$
y = \text{number of Form 4 pupils}
$$

Conditions:
- The total number of participants is at most $40$.
- The number of Form 5 pupils is not less than the number of Form 4 pupils.
- At least $15$ Form 4 pupils participate.

Translate each condition:

At most $40$ participants:

$$
x + y \leq 40
$$

Form 5 pupils are not less than Form 4 pupils:

$$
x \geq y
$$

At least $15$ Form 4 pupils:

$$
y \geq 15
$$

Therefore, the system is:

$$
x + y \leq 40
$$

$$
x \geq y
$$

$$
y \geq 15
$$

### Worked Example:
A computer shop sells refill ink of brand $X$ and brand $Y$.

Let:

$$
x = \text{number of brand } X \text{ refill ink}
$$

$$
y = \text{number of brand } Y \text{ refill ink}
$$

Conditions:
- The total number ordered does not exceed $25$ units.
- The number of brand $Y$ ink is at least twice the number of brand $X$ ink.
- The number of brand $Y$ ink is not more than $8$ units.
- Brand $X$ costs RM$14$ and brand $Y$ costs RM$18$.
- The total purchase does not exceed RM$400$.

Translate each condition:

Total units:

$$
x + y \leq 25
$$

Brand $Y$ is at least twice brand $X$:

$$
y \geq 2x
$$

Brand $Y$ is not more than $8$:

$$
y \leq 8
$$

Total cost:

$$
14x + 18y \leq 400
$$

Since the variables are quantities:

$$
x \geq 0,\quad y \geq 0
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- “At least twice” means $\geq 2x$.
- “At most three times” means $\leq 3x$.
- “Does not exceed” means $\leq$.
- Remember to include non-negative constraints when the variables represent quantities.

## Concept: Verifying a Region for a System of Linear Inequalities
A region satisfies a system of inequalities if all points in that region satisfy all the inequalities.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Choose a test point from each region.
- Substitute the point into every inequality.
- If the point satisfies all inequalities, the region is part of the solution.
- If one inequality is false, the region is not the solution region.

### Worked Example:
Determine the region that satisfies:

$$
x + y \leq 8
$$

and

$$
y \geq 3
$$

Test point $(2,5)$:

For $x + y \leq 8$:

$$
2 + 5 \leq 8
$$

$$
7 \leq 8
$$

This is true.

For $y \geq 3$:

$$
5 \geq 3
$$

This is true.

Therefore, the region containing $(2,5)$ satisfies both inequalities.

Test point $(2,1)$:

For $x + y \leq 8$:

$$
2 + 1 \leq 8
$$

$$
3 \leq 8
$$

This is true.

For $y \geq 3$:

$$
1 \geq 3
$$

This is false.

Therefore, the region containing $(2,1)$ does not satisfy the system.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- A region must satisfy every inequality in the system.
- Testing only one inequality is not enough.
- Choose simple points from the region whenever possible.

## Concept: Shading the Region of a System of Linear Inequalities
The solution region of a system of inequalities is the common region that satisfies all inequalities.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Draw the boundary line for each inequality.
- Use dashed lines for $>$ and $<$.
- Use solid lines for $\geq$ and $\leq$.
- Mark or shade the region for each inequality.
- Identify the common region.
- Shade the common region completely.
- Make sure the final shading is not outside the common region.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region satisfying:

$$
y \leq x + 3
$$

and

$$
x < 2
$$

Step $1$: Draw the line:

$$
y = x + 3
$$

Since the inequality is $\leq$, use a solid line and shade below the line.

Step $2$: Draw the line:

$$
x = 2
$$

Since the inequality is $<$, use a dashed vertical line and shade to the left of the line.

Step $3$: The solution is the common region that is both below $y = x + 3$ and to the left of $x = 2$.

### Worked Example:
Shade the region satisfying:

$$
y \geq -x
$$

$$
y < 3
$$

$$
y \geq 0
$$

Step $1$: Draw:

$$
y = -x
$$

Since $y \geq -x$, use a solid line and shade above the line.

Step $2$: Draw:

$$
y = 3
$$

Since $y < 3$, use a dashed horizontal line and shade below the line.

Step $3$: Draw:

$$
y = 0
$$

Since $y \geq 0$, use a solid horizontal line and shade above the $x$-axis.

Step $4$: The solution is the common region satisfying all three inequalities.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not shade only one inequality.
- The final answer must be the overlapping region.
- If the graph represents real quantities, draw only in the first quadrant if appropriate.
- The $x$-axis has equation $y = 0$.
- The $y$-axis has equation $x = 0$.

## Concept: Writing Inequalities from a Shaded Region
A shaded region can be described using linear inequalities.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Identify all boundary lines.
- Find the equation of each boundary line.
- Decide whether the shaded region is above, below, left, or right of each line.
- Decide whether each boundary line is included.
- Use:
  - $>$ or $<$ for dashed lines.
  - $\geq$ or $\leq$ for solid lines.

### Worked Example:
A shaded region is bounded by:
- The line $x + y = 8$.
- The $x$-axis.
- The $y$-axis.

The shaded region is below the line $x + y = 8$, above the $x$-axis, and to the right of the $y$-axis.

If the line $x + y = 8$ is dashed:

$$
x + y < 8
$$

Above the $x$-axis:

$$
y \geq 0
$$

To the right of the $y$-axis:

$$
x \geq 0
$$

Therefore, the inequalities are:

$$
x + y < 8
$$

$$
y \geq 0
$$

$$
x \geq 0
$$

### Worked Example:
A shaded region is bounded by:
- The line $y = 5$.
- The line $y = x$.
- The $y$-axis.

The shaded region is below $y = 5$, above $y = x$, and to the right of the $y$-axis.

Therefore:

$$
y \leq 5
$$

$$
y \geq x
$$

$$
x \geq 0
$$

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Find the equation of each boundary line first.
- Use the shaded side to decide the inequality sign.
- Check whether the boundary is dashed or solid.
- Do not forget inequalities for the axes if they form part of the boundary.

## Concept: Solving Problems Involving Systems of Linear Inequalities
Systems of linear inequalities can be used to solve real-life problems involving selection, cost, capacity, production, and planning.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Define the variables.
- Write inequalities for each condition.
- Include $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ if the variables represent quantities.
- Draw the graph and shade the common region.
- Use the common region to answer questions about maximum or minimum values.
- Check whether a given pair of values lies inside the common region.

### Worked Example:
Madam Carol needs to select at most $20$ pupils for a choir competition.

The number of girls is at least twice the number of boys.

Let:

$$
x = \text{number of girls}
$$

$$
y = \text{number of boys}
$$

Condition $1$: At most $20$ pupils.

$$
x + y \leq 20
$$

Condition $2$: The number of girls is at least twice the number of boys.

$$
x \geq 2y
$$

Since $x$ and $y$ represent numbers of pupils:

$$
x \geq 0,\quad y \geq 0
$$

If there are $5$ boys, then:

$$
y = 5
$$

Using $x \geq 2y$:

$$
x \geq 2(5)
$$

$$
x \geq 10
$$

Using $x + y \leq 20$:

$$
x + 5 \leq 20
$$

$$
x \leq 15
$$

Therefore, when there are $5$ boys, the number of girls is from $10$ to $15$.

Minimum number of girls:

$$
10
$$

Maximum number of girls:

$$
15
$$

If $8$ boys want to participate:

$$
y = 8
$$

The graph shows this value is outside the common region, so the condition is not satisfied.

### Worked Example:
The maximum number of passengers on a train in a theme park is $30$.

The number of adults must be greater than or equal to the number of children.

Let:

$$
x = \text{number of children}
$$

$$
y = \text{number of adults}
$$

Condition $1$: Maximum passengers is $30$.

$$
x + y \leq 30
$$

Condition $2$: Adults are greater than or equal to children.

$$
y \geq x
$$

Also:

$$
x \geq 0,\quad y \geq 0
$$

From the graph, the maximum number of children is $15$.

If there are $18$ children, they cannot all board at the same time because the maximum number of children allowed by the common region is $15$.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The graph is usually drawn in the first quadrant when variables represent quantities.
- Read maximum and minimum values from the common region.
- A point outside the common region does not satisfy the conditions.
- Always justify answers using the inequalities or the graph.

## Concept: Common Region
The common region is the region that satisfies all inequalities in a system.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- It is the overlapping region after all inequalities are shaded.
- Every point in the common region is a solution.
- A point outside the common region does not satisfy at least one inequality.
- In real-life problems, the answer must usually be taken from the common region.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not choose a point just because it satisfies one condition.
- The common region must satisfy all conditions at the same time.
- If the variables represent whole objects, final answers should be whole numbers.

## Concept: First Quadrant Restrictions
Many real-life inequality problems are restricted to the first quadrant.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- If $x$ and $y$ represent quantities, then:

$$
x \geq 0
$$

and

$$
y \geq 0
$$

- This is because quantities such as number of pupils, number of items, length, and cost cannot be negative.
- The graph should usually be drawn only in the first quadrant for such problems.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Do not include negative values for real-life quantities.
- Remember to add $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$ when required.
- If the question says “other than $x \geq 0$ and $y \geq 0$”, do not list them as part of the requested answer.

## Concept: Reflection of Shaded Regions
A shaded region may be reflected in the $x$-axis or $y$-axis.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Reflection in the $x$-axis changes the sign of the $y$-coordinate.
- Reflection in the $y$-axis changes the sign of the $x$-coordinate.
- After reflection, write the new boundary equations and inequalities.
- Check the new shaded side carefully.

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- Reflection in the $x$-axis affects $y$, not $x$.
- Reflection in the $y$-axis affects $x$, not $y$.
- After reflecting, re-check whether the shaded region is above, below, left, or right of each boundary.

## Concept: Linear Inequalities and Optimisation
Linear inequalities are related to optimisation problems, especially in business and planning.

**Key Rules / Methods:**
- Linear inequalities can describe limited resources such as:
  - Budget
  - Time
  - Number of workers
  - Materials
  - Capacity
- A system of linear inequalities can be used to find a feasible region.
- The feasible region contains possible solutions.
- Optimisation involves choosing the best solution, such as:
  - Maximum profit
  - Minimum cost
  - Maximum production
  - Minimum resources

### Common Mistakes & Exam Tips:
- The feasible region is not always the final answer.
- Use the condition in the question to decide which point or range is required.
- In word problems, explain the answer in context.
